## 1. People (Historical Figures, Royalty, Officials, Scholars, and Military Commanders)

### Javanese Royalty and Princely Families
*   **Dipanagara, Prince (Pangéran Dipanagara)**: Central figure of the Java War (1825–1830) and primary subject of the book. Childhood names: Bendara Radèn Mas Mustahar, Radèn Antawirya. Religious/wandering names: Sèh Ngabdurahim, Ngabdulkamit. Assumed the title of the Javanese messianic *Ratu Adil* ('Just King') and styled himself Sultan Ngabdulkamid Èrucakra Sayidin Panatagama Kalifat Rasulullah, Kabiril Mukminin, and Kalipah Rasulullah Jawa in his exile letters.
*   **Sultan Mangkubumi (Hamengkubuwana I)**: First Sultan of Yogyakarta (reigned 1749–1792), founder of the sultanate, and great-grandfather of Dipanagara.
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana II (Sultan Sepuh / Gusti Radèn Mas Sundoro)**: Second Sultan of Yogyakarta (reigned 1792–1810, 1811–1812, 1826–1828), grandfather of Dipanagara. Head of the *kasepuhan* faction. Deposed and exiled twice (Pulau Pinang/Ambon).
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana III (Raja Putra Naréndra / Pangéran Adipati Anom / Kanjeng Rama)**: Third Sultan of Yogyakarta (reigned 1812–1814), father of Dipanagara. Leader of the *karajan* faction, elevated to Sultan by the British.
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV (Sultan Séda Besiyar / Jarot)**: Fourth Sultan of Yogyakarta (reigned 1814–1822), younger half-brother of Dipanagara. Died suddenly in 1822.
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana V**: Child Sultan of Yogyakarta (reigned 1822–1826, 1828–1855), nephew of Dipanagara, placed under a regency/guardianship.
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana VI (Pangéran Mangkubumi)**: Younger brother of HB V, reigned 1855–1877.
*   **Sultan Hamengkubuwana VII, VIII, IX**: Later Sultans of Yogyakarta (HB IX made historical materials available).
*   **Sunan Pakubuwana IV (Sunan Bagus)**: Ruler of Surakarta (reigned 1788–1820) who secretly conspired with the Sepoys and Yogya against the British.
*   **Sunan Pakubuwana V**: Son of PB IV, reigned 1820–1823.
*   **Sunan Pakubuwana VI (Radèn Mas Saperdan)**: Reigned 1823–1830. Mercurial ruler who secretly sympathized with Dipanagara and was exiled to Ambon in 1830.
*   **Sunan Pakubuwana VII (Pangéran Purbaya)**: Reigned 1830–1858.
*   **Sunan Pakubuwana VIII**: Reigned 1858–1861.
*   **Pangéran Natakusuma (Pakualam I)**: Son of HB I, exiled by Daendels but installed as independent prince of the Pakualaman by Raffles (reigned 1812–1829).
*   **Radèn Tumenggung Natadiningrat (Pakualam II)**: Son of Natakusuma, reigned 1829–1858.
*   **Pangéran Suryasasraningrat (Pakualam III)**: Reigned 1858–1864.
*   **Radèn Mas Said (Mangkunagara I / Sultan Adiprakasa Lala Jayamisésa)**: Legendary 18th-century guerrilla fighter and founder of the Mangkunagaran line.
*   **Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II)**: Reigned 1796–1835. Created the Mangkunagaran Legion under Daendels and allied with the British/Dutch.
*   **Mangkunagara III**: Reigned 1835–1853.
*   **Ratu Ageng Tegalreja (Ratu Kadipatèn / Mas Rara Juwati)**: Great-grandmother of Dipanagara, widow of HB I. Devout Muslim who raised Dipanagara at Tegalreja.
*   **Ratu Kedhaton (later Ratu Ageng)**: Official wife of HB II, mother of HB III, and grandmother of Dipanagara. Stepmother/rival to HB III's circle.
*   **Ratu Kencana Wulan (later Ratu Wétan)**: Powerful, wealthy, and politically dominant favorite consort of HB II.
*   **Ratu Kencana (later Ratu Ibu / Ratu Ageng)**: Mother of HB IV who allied with Danureja IV and Major Wiranagara against Dipanagara.
*   **Radèn Ayu Mangkarawati**: Secondary wife of HB III and mother of Dipanagara (recipient of 1830 letter).

### Javanese Officials, Commanders, and Local Leaders (Appendix 5b & 8)

## 2. Places (Geographical Locations, Key Sites, Tollgates, Pathok Negari, & Country Estates)

*   **Java**: Primary setting, specifically south-central Java.
*   **Yogyakarta (Yogya / Djocja / Pasar Yogya)**: Capital of the Sultanate & central market tax farm.
*   **Surakarta (Solo / Sala)**: Capital of the Sunanate.
*   **Batavia (Jakarta)**: Center of Dutch colonial administration and site of Dipanagara's 1830 stay in the Stadhuis.
*   **Semarang**: Main northern coastal port and administrative seat for Java's Northeast Coast.
*   **Surabaya**: Main naval port, commercial hub, and location of the Tawangsari copper mint.
*   **Tegalreja**: Country estate west of Yogya where Dipanagara was raised, destroyed on 20 July 1825.
*   **Sélareja**: Dipanagara's personal meditation retreat at Tegalreja (Pulo Waringin).
*   **Selarong**: Dipanagara's retreat and first military headquarters in Bantul, featuring the cave of Secang.
*   **Guwa Secang**: Cave at Selarong where Dipanagara received his vision of the 8 *Wali Wudhar*.
*   **Madiun**: Capital of the eastern *mancanagara*, domain of Radèn Rongga III, and major tollgate farm.
*   **Maospati**: Radèn Rongga's fortified *dalem* (residence) west of Madiun.
*   **Taman (Madiun)**: Burial place of Radèn Rongga Prawiradirja I.
*   **Giripurna (Gunung Bancak)**: Burial place of Radèn Rongga III's wife Ratu Maduretna.
*   **Bayem (Madiun)**: Burial place of Kyai Tumenggung Resagota.
*   **Kalibeber**: District in Kedhu/Wonosobo; domain of R.Ng. Danuwijaya.
*   **Kawisarja**: Village in Kulon Praga where Radèn Ayu Maduretna (Ratu Kedhaton) died in February 1828.
*   **Kutha Pethik / Ketangga**: Area near Ngawi prophesied in Jayabaya texts as the site of the *Ratu Adil*'s kingdom.
*   **Sekaran**: Village on the Sala River where Radèn Rongga and Sumanegara were killed in December 1810.
*   **Banyusumurup**: The "traitors' graveyard" southeast of Imagiri where Rongga, Danukusuma I, and others were buried; also a minor tollgate farm.
*   **Magelang (Pasar Magelang)**: Capital of Kedhu Residency and site of Dipanagara's betrayal and arrest by De Kock on 28 March 1830; also market site.
*   **Manado**: Site of Dipanagara's initial exile (1830–1833), housed in Fort New Amsterdam.
*   **Makasar (Makassar / Fort Rotterdam / Kampung Melayu)**: Site of Dipanagara's final exile, family grave, and death (1833–1855).
*   **Tondano / Tonsea Lama (Kampung Jawa Tondano)**: Site in Minahasa where Kyai Maja and his followers were exiled (established across the Tondano River after 1831).
*   **Bagelèn**: The western *nagara agung*, includes weaving districts like Tanggung and Rèma, and major tollgate farms.
*   **Rèma (Rèmakamal)**: Mountainous district between Bagelèn and Banyumas where Dipanagara hid and negotiated with Cleerens at Rèmakamal and Kecawang; also a tollgate farm.
*   **Kecawang**: Village in northern Bagelèn where Cleerens negotiated with Dipanagara in February 1830.
*   **Royal Military Academy at Breda**: Archive location in Holland where Dipanagara's 1830 Batavia letters were preserved.
*   **Banyumas**: The western *mancanagara*.
*   **Gamping**: Limestone quarries, site of HB I's pre-Yogya *pesanggrahan*, and major tollgate farm west of Yogya.
*   **Taman Sari (Water Palace)**: Royal pleasure garden and gun foundry in Yogya (Pulo Kenonga & Pulo Gedhong).
*   **Meester Cornelis (Jatinegara)**: Daendels' heavily fortified strongpoint near Batavia, captured by the British in August 1811.
*   **Klathèn**: Midway town between Yogya and Surakarta where inter-court treaties were signed.
*   **Pacitan (Pasar Pacitan)**: Remote south coast district targeted for pepper, annexed by the British in 1812; also market tax farm.
*   **Grindulu River**: River valley in Pacitan.
*   **Sendhang Pitu**: Region between Slèman and Kali Praga.
*   **Imagiri**: Sacred royal graveyard of the Mataram dynasty.
*   **Bengkung**: Pond and retreat site at the top of the Imagiri stairway.
*   **Song Kamal**: Cave in Jejeran district where Dipanagara received his first vision in c. 1805.
*   **Guwa Séluman**: Cave near the Oyo River, home to the spirit Genawati; also a royal retreat built by HB II.
*   **Guwa Suracala (Guwa Sigala-gala)**: Cave on the Kali Opak in Gunung Kidul.
*   **Guwa Langsé**: Sheer coastal cave where Dipanagara meditated and met Ratu Kidul.
*   **Parangkusuma / Parangtritis / Parangwédang / Mancingan**: Sacred sites on the southern coast dedicated to Ratu Kidul.
*   **Sragèn**: Ancestral home of Ratu Ageng in Pajang.
*   **Majasta**: Pradikan village near Tembayat, birthplace of Dipanagara's mother.
*   **Tembayat**: Famous religious center in the Pajang area.
*   **Melangi**: Pathok negari (center for Islamic law experts) near Yogya.
*   **Dhadhapan**: Village near Tempèl, home of Dipanagara's first wife.
*   **Panolan & Jipang**: Eastern *mancanagara* districts crucial for their teak forests, annexed in 1812.
*   **Padhangan**: Eastern *mancanagara* district administered by Sumanegara.
*   **Bunder**: Chinese customs post on the Brantas River.
*   **Weleri**: Spot on the post road where Radèn Tirtawijaya was executed.
*   **Kali Tuntang**: River and site of Janssens' capitulation to the British on 18 September 1811.
*   **Nusa Kambangan & Cilacap**: Deepwater anchorages on the south coast.
*   **Sumbawa (Bima)**: Islamic principality linked to Ratu Ageng's maternal ancestry.
*   **Lipura**: Site containing *Séla Gilang*, a holy black stone associated with Panembahan Sénapati.
*   **Durrumtollah Academy**: School in Calcutta where sons of Sura-adimanggala IV studied.
*   **Panolan, Padhangan, Rajegwesi, Sekaran, Dhuri, Bauwerna**: Component districts of Jipang annexed by the British in 1812.
*   **Candhi Sèwu, Prambanan, Kalasan**: Ancient Hindu/Buddhist temple complexes east of Yogya.
*   **Bedhaya**: Nahuys' country estate on the flanks of Mt. Merapi.
*   **Rajawinangun / Arjawinangun**: Royal estate leased to Dr. Harvey Thomson for indigo production.
*   **Mount Rasamuni / Mount Lawu / Mount Merapi / Mount Sumbing / Mount Sundoro**: Volcanic peaks associated with spiritual visions and eruptions (December 1822).
*   **Jabarangkah & Karangkobar**: Districts annexed by the Dutch on 1 January 1825.

## 3. Terms, Concepts, Texts, Weapons, Military Units, and Glossary Definitions

### Cultural, Political, Legal, and Religious Concepts & Glossary Definitions
*   **abdi-Dalem**: Royal official or retainer living within or near the kraton walls.
*   **adat**: Custom, time-honored practice in social etiquette and legal matters.
*   **adipati**: Title used with *pangéran* indicating a senior prince with an extensive apanage.
*   **ali basah**: Title ('High Pasha', from Turkish *Ali Pasha*) given by Dipanagara to select army commanders.
*   **alun-alun**: The great open square in front of and behind the kraton, planted with boxed waringin trees.
*   **Apanage (Lungguh / gadhuhan)**: Royal land grant given to princes and officials in lieu of salary.
*   **babad**: Javanese historical chronicle written in verse cantos.
*   **bandar**: Keeper of a tollgate or customs post (usually Chinese tax-farmers).
*   **batik**: Intricately designed wax-dyed fabric.
*   **batur**: Porter or coolie carrying shoulder loads (*pikul*).
*   **bekel**: Local village tax collector operating on behalf of apanage holders.
*   **bènthèng stelsel**: System of temporary battlefield fortifications used by De Kock to defeat Dipanagara.
*   **blandhong**: Workers in the royal teak forests.
*   **bupati / kabupatèn**: High administrative official / district residence and office of a bupati.
*   **cacah**: Peasant household unit used for measuring land productivity and population (approx. 5 people).
*   **dalem**: Princely residence or home of a high court official featuring a *pendhapa*.
*   **demang**: Middle-ranking provincial tax and police official (*mantri-désa*).
*   **désa**: Village complex with attached ricefields and orchards.
*   **dhalang**: Puppeteer in the wayang shadow theatre.
*   **emban**: Guardian or nurse of a young child or youth below sixteen.
*   **Èrucakra**: Title of the Javanese messianic 'Just King' (*Ratu Adil*).
*   **gamelan**: Traditional Javanese percussion and wind orchestra.
*   **Garebeg (Mulud / Puwasa / Besar)**: Thrice-annual Javanese-Islamic court festivals celebrating the Prophet's Birthday, the end of Ramadhan, and the pilgrimage to Mecca.
*   **gunung**: Mountain or large hill; also title for village police and tax officials.
*   **guwa**: Cave or grotto (e.g., Guwa Secang, Guwa Séluman, Guwa Langsé).
*   **haji / haj**: Title of a returned pilgrim from Mecca / the pilgrimage to Mecca.
*   **jago**: Bandit chieftain, thug, or underworld enforcer ('fighting cock').
*   **jaman édan / kaliyuga**: "Time of madness" / final age of disorder preceding the *Ratu Adil*.
*   **jimat**: Amulet, talisman, or heirloom believed to ward off sickness and provide invulnerability.
*   **jung**: Javanese unit of land measurement (approx. 4 *bau* or 2.8 hectares).
*   **jurukunci / jurukuncèn**: Doorkeeper or guardian of the keys of a royal grave or holy site / grave guardians' land.
*   **kadipatèn**: Official residence of the Crown Prince (*Pangéran Adipati Anom*).
*   **kali**: Large river (e.g., Kali Praga, Kali Opak, Bengawan Sala).
*   **kampung**: Village or urban settlement quarter (e.g., Kampung Cina, Kampung Melayu).
*   **kapir laknatullah / kapir murtad**: "Heretics accursed by Allah" (Europeans) / "Apostates" (Javanese allied with Dutch).
*   **kapitan Cina**: Head of the Chinese community in a town or provincial area.
*   **kasepuhan / karajan**: The 'old' court faction (HB II) vs. the 'young' Crown Prince faction (HB III).
*   **kauman**: Strict religious community in court cities living near the Great Mosque (*Mesjid Ageng*).
*   **kebathinan**: Javanese spiritual and mystical disciplines (science of the inner self).
*   **kècu / wong durjana / kraman**: Robber, highwayman, bandit / 'evil folk' / rebel.
*   **kelangenan-Dalem / pesanggrahan**: Royal pleasure gardens, hunting lodges, and country estates.
*   **kepatihan**: Office and residence of the chief minister (*patih*).
*   **krama / ngoko**: High Javanese (formal) vs. Low Javanese (informal).
*   **kraton**: Fortified royal palace, court, and residence of a ruler.
*   **kris**: Javanese stabbing dagger with meteoritic iron damascening (*pamor*).
*   **kyai**: Honorific title for revered older men, country gentlemen, Islamic scholars, or heirloom weapons.
*   **labuhan**: Ceremonial offering thrown into the sea to Ratu Kidul.
*   **lakon**: Plot or story enacted in a wayang performance.
*   **lelana / tirakat / tapa**: Spiritual wanderings, ascetic withdrawal, and meditation.
*   **lurah**: Chief or head of a village or administrative branch.
*   **mancanagara**: Outer provinces of the kingdom (eastern and western).
*   **mantri**: Lower-ranking official in the royal administration.
*   **mas / radèn mas / radèn**: Titles of Javanese nobility.
*   **mesjid**: Mosque or place of worship (*Mesjid Ageng* = Great Mosque).
*   **nagara agung**: Core royal territories surrounding the capital.
*   **nayaka**: Senior court adviser / member of the eight-man council of state.
*   **ngindhung / numpang**: Village dependants with house yards vs. landless lodgers/day laborers.
*   **pajeg**: Land-rent or tribute paid to apanage holders.
*   **pamutihan / wong putihan**: Tax-free religious settlements / "people dressed in white" (pious Muslims).
*   **panakawan**: Intimate retainers/advisers of a ruler (e.g., Banthèngwarèng, Sahiman, Rata, Kasimun, Teplak, Rujakbeling).
*   **pancas**: Fiscal revision reducing the size of the *jung* while maintaining tax levels.
*   **pangrembé**: Special domain lands assigned directly to the ruler for revenue.
*   **panembahan / pangéran / panji**: High princely title / prince / young nobleman or military commander.
*   **pandhita-ratu**: Priest-king exercising both spiritual and temporal power.
*   **pasisir**: Coastal regions, particularly the north coast of Java.
*   **pathok negari**: Centers for Islamic law experts (*ulama*) acting as advisers to the *pengulu* (Dhongkèlan, Melangi, Papringan, Plasajené, Purwareja, Kasongan).
*   **patih / patih jaba / patih jero / patih séda kedhaton**: Chief minister / prime minister for external affairs / internal court administrator / "patih who died in the kraton" (Danureja II).
*   **payung**: State umbrella used as insignia of rank.
*   **pégon**: Javanese written in unvocalised Arabic script.
*   **pendhapa**: Large covered audience hall in front of a Javanese house.
*   **pengulu**: Head of the religious hierarchy in court towns and judge in the *surambi* court.
*   **pesanggrahan**: Royal pavilion, overnight stopping place, or hunting lodge.
*   **pesantrèn / pondhok**: Religious boarding school for students of Islam.
*   **pikul**: Shoulder-load carrying weight (approx. 62 kgs / 136 lbs).
*   **pradikan / pradikan ageng**: Tax-free village set aside for religious pursuits or graveyard guardians.
*   **prajurit / prajurit keparak èstri / prajurit arahan**: Palace soldier / Amazon corps / provincial levies.
*   **prang sabil**: Holy war waged for Islamic religious ideals.
*   **priyayi**: Official class in south-central Java ("younger brothers of the ruler").
*   **pusaka**: Holy regalia or sacred family heirloom.
*   **ratu / ratu adil / Ratu Kidul / Ratu Kedhaton**: Principal official wife of a ruler / messianic 'Just King' / Goddess of the Southern Ocean / title given to Dipanagara's wife Radèn Ayu Maduretna in August 1825.
*   **santri / wong putihan**: Pious Muslim student or follower / "people dressed in white".
*   **sawah / tegal**: Wet irrigated ricefield vs. dry crop field.
*   **sèh (syèh)**: Title for high religious dignitaries and Arab scholars.
*   **selir**: Secondary or unofficial wife of a prince or ruler.
*   **sénapati**: Commander-in-Chief of an army.
*   **sengkala**: Chronogram date or list.
*   **sentana**: Royal family member or distant relative.
*   **Service Malay**: Bureaucratic colonial language derided by Dipanagara as "language of chickens."
*   **sikep (kuli sikep)**: Tax-paying peasant cultivator holding village land.
*   **strandgeld**: Dutch compensation money paid for control of the north coast.
*   **tanah tiyasa**: Family-endowed tax-free religious lands.
*   **tembang**: Scanned verse used in Javanese chronicles.
*   **tumenggung**: Title of high administrative rank given to bupati.
*   **ulama**: Islamic scholars learned in jurisprudence (*fiqh*).
*   **wakil / wakil-dalem**: Guardian or regent representing a minor ruler (e.g., Pangéran Mangkubumi for HB V).
*   **wali / Wali Sanga / Wali Wudhar**: Apostles of Islam / Nine Apostles of Java / Dual spiritual-temporal apostle.
*   **wayang (kulit, purwa, wong, topèng)**: Javanese puppet/dance theatre.
*   **wedana**: Senior administrative official (*bupati wedana*).
*   **wong cilik**: Common people.

### Chronology, Calendar Concepts, and Year Cycles (Appendix 14)
*   **AJ (Anno Javano)**: The Javanese lunar calendar era.
*   **AH (Anno Hijrae)**: The Muslim calendar era.
*   **AD (Anno Domini)**: The Western Gregorian calendar era.
*   **Windu Cycle**: An eight-year lunar calendar cycle in the Javanese system.
*   **Eight Windu Years**: *Alip* (1), *Éhé* (2), *Jimawal* (3), *Jé* (4), *Dal* (5), *Bé* (6), *Wawu* (7), *Jimakir* (8).
*   **Twelve Javanese Months**:
    1. *Sura* (*Muharam*)
    2. *Sapar*
    3. *Mulud* (*Rabingulawal*)
    4. *Bakda Mulud* (*Rabingulakir*)
    5. *Jumadilawal*
    6. *Jumadilakir*
    7. *Rejeb*
    8. *Ruwah* (*Saban*)
    9. *Puwasa* (*Ramelan / Pasa*)
    10. *Sawal*
    11. *Dulkangidah* (*Séla / Apit*)
    12. *Besar* (*Dulkijah*)
*   **Pasaran (Javanese 5-day Market Week)**: *Legi*, *Pahing*, *Pon*, *Wage*, *Kliwon* (e.g., *Jumungah Wagé*).

### Literary, Historical, and Religious Texts
*   **Babad Dipanagara (Manado version)**: Dipanagara's monumental autobiography written in exile in Manado (1831–1832).
*   **Babad Dipanagara (Surakarta version)**: Surakarta-authored chronicle of the Java War.
*   **Babad Dipanagaran Surya Ngalam**: Allegorical historical chronicle written in exile by Dipanagara II.
*   **Babad Bedhah ing Ngayogyakarta**: Chronicle of the fall and looting of Yogyakarta in 1812.
*   **Buku Kedhung Kebo**: Historical chronicle authored by Ali Basah Kerta Pengalasan and Radèn Adipati Danureja V.
*   **Babad Kraton, Babad Ngayogyakarta, Babad Pacitan, Babad Pakualam, Babad Tanah Jawi**: Major Javanese historical chronicles.
*   **Serat Centhini & Serat Cabolang**: Monumental 19th-century encyclopedic Javanese texts commissioned by Sunan PB V.
*   **Serat Raja Kapa-Kapa, Jugul Mudha, Surya Ngalam, Praniti Kapa-Kapa**: Javanese law codes and administrative manuals.
*   **Taqrīb, Taqarrub, Lubāb al-fiqh, Muharrar, Uşul, Tafsir**: Foundational texts on Muslim jurisprudence and theology.
*   **Kitab Tuhfah (Al-Tuhfa al-mursala ilā rūh al-Nabī)**: Sufi ontology on the 'seven grades of being'.
*   **Tāj as-Salāţīn & Sirāt as-Salāţīn**: Classical Islamic mirrors for princes and political philosophy manuals.
*   **Bustān as-Salaţīn, Fateh al-Muluk, Naşīhat al-Muluk, Hikayat Makutha Raja**: Didactic texts on statecraft.
*   **Serat Rama, Serat Gandakusuma, Arjuna Wiwāha, Bhoma Kāwya, Arjuna Sasrabahu, Serat Suryaraja, Serat Ambiya, Sejarah Sétan lan Jim, Puja Kusuma, Angling Driya, Asmara Supi, Manikmaya, Ménak Amir Hamzah**: Epic, literary, and wayang texts.
*   **Sejarah Ratu Tanah Jawa & Hikayat Tanah Jawa**: Dipanagara's prose manuscripts written in exile in Makassar (1838).
*   **Serat Salasilah Para Leluhur ing Kadanurejan**: Genealogical chronicle of the Danurejan family.

### Heirloom Weapons (Pusaka)
*   **Kangjeng Kyai Ageng Bandayuda**: Dipanagara's primary heirloom dagger carried into exile, melted down from three pusaka (*Sarutama*, *Barutuba*, *Abijaya*), and buried with him in Makassar.
*   **Kangjeng Kyai Naga Siluman**: Dipanagara's famous kris ("Magician King of the Snakes") presented to King William I of the Netherlands in 1831.
*   **Kyai Sarutama**: Dagger/arrow (*cundrik*) received by Dipanagara at Parangkusuma c. 1805.
*   **Kyai Barutuba**: Heirloom pike carried by Ngusman Ali Basah, commander of the Bulkio regiment.
*   **Kyai Abijaya**: Inherited pusaka kris worn by Dipanagara at Tegalreja in July 1825.
*   **Kyai Wisa Bintulu**: Poisoned kraton kris requested back by Ratu Ageng in March 1820 due to prophecy of rule in Yogya.
*   **Kyai Taslim**: Master ironmaster/blacksmith (*ahli pethèk wesi pamor*) who evaluated the *Wisa Bintulu* kris.
*   **Kyai Blabar & Kyai Bromo Kedali**: Heirloom kris given to Dipanagara II in Magelang.
*   **Kyai Mundingwangi & Kyai Rondhan**: Heirloom pikes given to Dipanagara II in Magelang.
*   **Kyai Habit & Kyai Gagasono**: Heirloom kris and pike given to Radèn Mas Jonèd in Magelang.
*   **Kyai Dipayana**: Heirloom kris given to Radèn Ajeng Impun in Magelang.
*   **Kyai Bandung**: Heirloom kris given to Radèn Ajeng Munthèng in Magelang.
*   **Kyai Hatim & Kyai Simo**: Heirloom kris and pike given to Radèn Ayu Jayakusuma in Yogyakarta.
*   **Kyai Wresa Gemilar & Kyai Téja**: Heirloom kris and pike given to Radèn Ayu Mertanagara in Yogyakarta.
*   **Kyai Setoma & Nyai Setomi**: Famous court pusaka cannons.
*   **Kyai Gupita, Kyai Jingga, Kyai Betha, Kyai Birawa, Kyai Sangkelat, Kyai Kebo Kemili**: Historic court heirloom weapons.

### Military Regiments, Units, Ships, and Forts
*   **Regiments & Bodyguard Units**:
    *   *Bulkio*, *Turkio*, *Arkio*, *Barjumungah*, *Suryagama*, *Suranatan*: Dipanagara's elite Java War regiments.
    *   *Prajurit keparak èstri* (Amazon corps): Javanese female royal bodyguards.
    *   *Ketanggung*, *Katanggel*, *Blambangan Sepuh*, *Daëng Secadipura*, *Trunasmara*: Kraton regiments.

    *   *6th, 7th, 11th Mobile Columns & 16th, 17th, 18th Garrison Companies*: Dutch military units during the Java War.
*   **Ships**:
    *   *SS Van der Capellen*: First steamship in Netherlands Indies service, used to transport Dipanagara to Batavia in 1830.
    *   *Corvette Pollux*: Dutch naval vessel that carried Dipanagara to Manado (May–June 1830).
    *   *Sloop Circe*: Naval schooner that carried Dipanagara to Makassar in 1833.

*   **Forts & Buildings**:
    *   *Fort Vredenburg (Rustenburg)*: Dutch fort opposite the Yogya kraton.

    *   *Fort Victoria (Ambon)*: Dutch fort in Maluku.

### Currencies, Tax Farms, Agricultural Price Terms, & Units (Appendix 10, 15 & 16)
*   **Rice & Agricultural Units**:
    *   *Pikul*: Shoulder load unit (61.761 kgs).
    *   *Padi & Beras*: Unhusked rice vs. milled/husked rice.
    *   *Bataviasche Courant*: Official government gazette publishing Java-wide rice quotations.
*   **Opium & Tax Farms**:
    *   *Opium Farm (Candhu / Madat)*: Monopoly tax farm for prepared opium in Yogyakarta and its districts.
    *   *Tollgates (Bandar / Rangkah / Salaran)*: Customs posts and subordinate collection stations along trade roads and rivers.
    *   *Pasar Tax Farms*: Market lease farms (e.g., Pasar Yogya, Pasar Pacitan, Pasar Payaman, Pasar Magelang).
    *   *Gedhong Tembakau*: Customs warehouses for tobacco (e.g., Sibebek, Gunung Tawang).
    *   *Buffalo Slaughtering Tax (Tugel Gurung)*: Municipal/court tax levied on slaughtered buffaloes.
*   **Currencies & Coinage**:
    *   *Rupiah mas*: Gold rupee coined in Batavia (Dfl 19.20).
    *   *Half rupiah mas*: Half gold rupee (Dfl 9.60).
    *   *Dutch / Venetian ducats*: Gold coins in general circulation (Dfl 5.76 / 4.32).
    *   *Rose noble (Gold ducat)*: Worth 11 guilders (£0 19s 9d).
    *   *Silver ducatoon*: Minted in Holland (Dfl 3.20 / 3 guilders).
    *   *Half silver ducatoon*: Minted in Holland (Dfl 1.60).
    *   *Spanish dollar (Real batu / Real de a ocho / SpD)*: Standard international silver coin (Dfl 2.56).
    *   *Portuguese cruzado / German crown (franca)*: Old mutilated silver coin (*real batu*) in general circulation (Dfl 2.40).
    *   *Rijksdaalder (Rix dollar)*: Silver coin / government paper currency (Dfl 2.40 / Dfl 1.92 discounted).
    *   *Surat rupee & Java rupee (rupiah / f)*: Standard silver coins in general circulation (Dfl 1.20).
    *   *Dutch guilder*: Silver coin (Dfl 0.80–1.20).
    *   *Half & Quarter Dutch guilder*: 6 stuivers (Dfl 0.30–0.60).
    *   *Austrian-Netherlands schilling*: Silver coin worth 6 stuivers (Dfl 0.24).
    *   *Dubbeltje*: Dutch 10 cents piece / 2½ stuivers (Dfl 0.10–0.15).
    *   *Wang baru & Tengah wang baru*: Poor quality copper coins produced at Tawangsari mint, Surabaya (2 stuivers = Dfl 0.10; 1 stuiver = Dfl 0.05).
    *   *Wang Bengal & Half wang Bengal*: Small round copper coins imported from Bengal (Dfl 0.04–0.08).
    *   *Duit (dhuwit)*: VOC copper coins in general village circulation (Dfl 0.02).
    *   *Sigar (Half duit)*: Half copper duit (Dfl 0.01).
    *   *Stuiver*: Dutch copper penny piece (Dfl 0.04 / 4 duit).
    *   *Penning*: 2 penning = 1 copper duit.
    *   *Agio*: Exchange premium of 15–20% between local coins and Holland-minted currency.
