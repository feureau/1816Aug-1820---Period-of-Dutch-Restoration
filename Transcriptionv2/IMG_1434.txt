Dear Peter,

As a kind of continuation of my interest in Hakka tin miners on Bangka, I have been looking at the gold miners in West Kalimantan, also for the most part Hakkas. Although this may seem a long way from Java, I have found some information I would like to ask your opinion about.

In 1818, Nahuys, who as you know had been Resident of Yogyakarta, was sent as Commissioner to Borneo. P.J. Veth in his Borneo's Wester-afdeeling (Vol II,p. 55-56) mentions that Nahuys was accompanied by a Chinese, born in Java, who had aided Daendels during his expedition to Yogyakarta and was given the Javanese title "Setja Adi-ningrat", raised to the rank of Temenggung, and given land. He was expected to act as an interpreter, and apparently performed good service. However, being a Hokkien Chinese and not a Hakka, he had a low opinion of Borneo's Chinese.

This person seems to be identical with "Tan Jin Sing". I found nothing in the biography of him by Werdoyo about his going to Borneo (small wonder), but I wonder if you ever came across any references to this excursion and could identify "Setjo Adi-ningrat" as Tan—or not.

This is a trivial matter, and I know you are more than busy with your own responsibilities. But I thought there might be a chance you would know if this person was the same as Tan Jin Sing or not. In any case, he certainly was a loyal servant of the Dutch.

If you are too busy to reply, or have no information, please just ignore my request.

Sincerely yours,
Mary
