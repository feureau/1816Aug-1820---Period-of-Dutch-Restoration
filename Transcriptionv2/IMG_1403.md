## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1403.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Port. 5, No. 11: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence Residence, Feb 1817)
- **Date Range:** 1817-02-17 to 1817-02-25
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Management of the Karangbolong edible birds' nest monopoly, official proclamation of Sultan Sepuh's overseas exile to the regents of Mataram, and the abolition of traditional judicial mutilation. On 17 February 1817, inquiries into the coastal birds' nest cliffs at Karangbolong revealed that maritime shipments to Batavia were unprofitable; harvests were rerouted overland via Semarang (packing and transit costs totaling 692 Rupees). Due to vulnerable terrain prone to armed bandit raids, Nahuys recommended stationing armed mantris and permanent pickets to secure the nests. On 25 February, Resident Nahuys summoned all bupatis of Mataram to the Yogyakarta Residency House, delivering an official address announcing the definitive banishment of Sultan Sepuh (Hamengkubuwana II) to Ambon. Concurrently, Patih Raden Adipati Danureja IV petitioned the Resident to sanction traditional corporal mutilation for twelve captured highway robbers (*kècu*), requesting that their hands and feet be hacked off. Nahuys emphatically refused, invoking Article 9 of the British-Javanese Treaty of 1812 which permanently abolished judicial mutilation, torture, and branding with red-hot irons across the Sultanate. Instead, convicted criminals and state prisoners were sentenced to overseas penal transport to Banjarmasin (Borneo) for forced labor on the private estate of Alexander Hare.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Raden Adipati Danureja IV [Patih], Sultan Hamengkubuwana II [Old Sultan / Sultan Sepuh], Alexander Hare, bupatis / regents of Mataram.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Karangbolong [Karang Bolong], Mataram, Yogyakarta [Djogja], Semarang, Ambon / Moluccas, Banjarmasin / Borneo, Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Edible birds' nests monopoly (*vogelnestencultuur*), treaty convention of 1812 (Article 9), judicial mutilation (*amputatie* / hacking off limbs), branding with red-hot irons, penal transportation (*dwangarbeid*), Alexander Hare's Banjarmasin concession, Mataram regency assembly, Java Rupees [J.R.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5, No. 11 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence, Februari 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Administrative inquiries, fiscal ledgers, ceremonial assembly minutes, and judicial determinations on penal law.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the clash between traditional Mataram corporal jurisprudence (mutilation of bandits) and European humanitarian legal norms codified under the British, alongside the integration of colonial monopolies (birds' nests) and penal labor systems across the Indonesian archipelago.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Grand Vizier Danureja IV, regional regents of Mataram, and private colonial concessionaires (Alexander Hare).
