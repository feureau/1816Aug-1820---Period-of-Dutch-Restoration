# Transcription of IMG_1420.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1420.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Portfolio 5, No. 11: Document A.9, Djokjakarta Letters, November–December 1817)
- **Date Range:** 1817-11-25 to 1817-12-28
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Finalization of the 1817 tollgate and bazaar tax reforms, auction of the joint Surakarta-Yogyakarta opium monopoly (*opiumpacht*), premature death of the infant Yogyakarta Crown Prince, and residency tax returns. In Document A.9, Resident Nahuys codified three decisive principles governing internal customs tollgates (*bandar*): (1) preservation of the tollgate mechanism but with strict prohibition against extracting higher sums from native Javanese than from Chinese merchants; (2) mandatory public posting of official tariff schedules in three languages (Javanese, Malay, and Chinese) at all toll stations across Surakarta and Yogyakarta territory; and (3) reducing the duration of tollgate leases from three years to two years to grant the colonial administration greater regulatory control over valuations. On 25 November 1817, preparations commenced for the annual public auction of the opium farm and tollgates held at the Residency House on 15 December. Severe market scarcity caused by the shipwreck of a cargo vessel from Bengal (chartered by Scott & Deans) inflated wholesale prices: raw Bengal opium surged to 1,500 Spanish Dollars per chest, while Turkish (Levant) opium reached 1,000 Spanish Dollars. Chinese Lieutenant Lie Tanjoe (Si Tonjoe) submitted a monopolistic bid to lease the combined opium farms of both Surakarta and Yogyakarta. On 25 December, negotiations concluded successfully renewing the farm at the previous year's rent, though Nahuys noted the operational absurdity of requiring farmers to travel personally to the capitals on the 10th of every month. On 26 December 1817, the newly born Crown Prince (born 8 September 1817) tragically died after a brief illness, plunging the Kraton of Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV into mourning. On 28 December, due to the severe illness of Resident Umbgrove, the Provisional Resident submitted the final 1817 taxation returns.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Lieutenant of the Chinese Lie Tanjoe [Si Tonjoe], Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, infant Crown Prince (deceased), Resident Umbgrove, Provisional Resident of Surakarta.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Bengal, Levant [Turkey].
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bandar* [customs tollgate], Trilingual tariff notices [Javanese, Malay, Chinese], *Opiumpacht* [opium monopoly tax farm], Bengal & Turkish opium, Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.], Scott & Deans shipping, Crown Prince death, annual tax returns.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 5, No. 11 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Collection, Document A.9, November–December 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Policy reform charter, tax-farm auction minutes, commercial commodity price reports, and court obituary notice.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Bridges high-stakes colonial revenue administration (tollgates and opium monopolies) with intimate Kraton tragedy, illustrating how Chinese fiscal syndicates dominated economic flows across the *Vorstenlanden*.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Chinese Lieutenant Lie Tanjoe, Resident Umbgrove, and the court of Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV.
* **Archival Header:** Top entries detail administrative policy points and archival markers (`Rent of toll-gates should be cut from 3 to 2 years / [red ink: (Doc. A.9) / 1817 Yogya]`).
* **Marginal Annotations:** Chronological markers and fiscal labels (`25 Nov 1817: TAX ON OPIUM`, `26 Dec 1817: DEATH of CROWN PRINCE`, `28 Dec 1817:`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, policy clauses, and opium types on the left; verbatim summaries of tollgate regulations, opium scarcity and auctions, dynastic death, and fiscal reports on the right.
