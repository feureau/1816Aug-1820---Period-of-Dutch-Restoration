# Transcription of IMG_1430.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1430.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (KITLV H 698b: Algemeen Verslag Jogja [AvJ], 1818: Fiscal Returns & Tollgate Surveys)
- **Date Range:** 1818-02-04 to 1818-12-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Macro-fiscal returns for Yogyakarta’s tollgates and municipal markets (*pasar*), geographic distribution analysis of twenty-four major customs barriers, quantitative revenue dominance of the eastern *manca nagara*, inquest into illegal river transit dues on the Bengawan Solo, and official currency exchange parity tables. For the second half of 1818 (1 July to 31 December), combined gross receipts from tollgates and markets totaled f. 92,015. The document provides an exhaustive inventory of twenty-four key toll stations: Juana, Kadilangu, Rama [Rêma], Brosot [Brasol], Wates [Wales], Gamping [Gampeng], Kemlaka, Bantul, Klètèk [Klêteg], Banaran, Boyolali, Kalasan, Borongan, Begaan [Bevaan], Jati Anom, Jerewan, Pantongan, Mawahan, Madiun, Rawa, Kalangbrèt, Kertosono [Kartesana], Punggur, and Pacitan, alongside the core Yogyakarta urban bazaars. Quantitative analysis reveals that the eastern *manca nagara* tollgate cluster (Madiun, Rawa, Kalangbrèt, Kertosono, and Punggur) generated fully one-quarter (25%) of total revenue, with the Madiun barrier alone producing one-eighth (12.5%) of the grand total—matching the combined receipts of Juana, Kemlaka, and Bantul. On 4 February 1818, an official *procès-verbal* investigated illicit river tolls levied on boat traffic along the Bengawan Solo under the pretext of traditional river passage fees (*wang palihan* or *wang playang*). The document concludes with standard colonial exchange conversions: 1 Spanish Dollar = f. 2.20; 1 silver Ducatoon = 2⅔ Indies guilders (f. 2.66); and ½ Ducatoon = 1⅓ Indies guilders (f. 1.33).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Chinese toll-farmers [Bandar], River prahu navigators, Patih Danureja IV.
