## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1457.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Kabinet Dossier No. 2065: Ingekomen Berigten over het Inlandsch Onderwijs, 1831–1832 / Tegal and Pekalongan Returns)
- **Date Range:** 1831-10-07 to 1832-12-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Educational assessments and priyayi reform initiatives in the Residencies of Tegal and Pekalongan, highlighting the legacy of the British interregnum in stimulating demand for secular literacy. On 3 November 1831, Resident of Tegal H. de Stürler reported that approximately 1,000 pupils attended Quranic pesantrens where teachers exclusively utilized Arabic script (*pegon*), leaving vernacular Javanese uncultivated. The Regent of Tegal strongly advised against expanding religious schools, advocating instead for secular primary schools in each of the three kabupatèns (Tegal, Brebes, and Pemalang) under direct regency oversight. The report notes that during the British interregnum (1811–1816), the colonial state's growing demand for native scribes and bilingual administrators had inspired high priyayi families to teach their sons Malay, Javanese, and Latin script (citing the Regent of Tegal's personal tutoring, referenced by Onghokham), noting that two other regional regents read Roman characters and actively championed state schooling. On 7 October 1831, Resident of Pekalongan A. de Bousquet surveyed native education, finding that common villagers lacked any institutional means to acquire Javanese vernacular literacy, cataloging nine prominent Islamic pesantrens across the residency: Kampong Pakauman, Jaspapura, Krapyak, and Kaputrian (in District Pekalongan); Desa Kaliwalak; Desa Kranji (Pakajangan); Pakauman (Wiradesa); and two centers in Batang.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Johannes van den Bosch, Resident of Tegal (H. de Stürler), Resident of Pekalongan (A. de Bousquet), Regent of Tegal, Regents of Brebes and Pemalang, Onghokham (historian citation), Thomas Stamford Raffles (English period reference).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Tegal, Brebes, Pemalang, Pekalongan [Pèkalongan], Batang, Wiradesa, Pakajangan, Krapyak, Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** British interregnum administrative impact, Priyayi literacy demands, Latin script acquisition, *Kabupatèn* school networks, Islamic pesantrens (9 centers in Pekalongan), *Pakauman* [mosque quarter], *Pegon* vs. Javanese script.
