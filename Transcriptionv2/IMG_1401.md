# Transcription of IMG_1401.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1401.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence General, Feb 1817: Report to the Governor-General)
- **Date Range:** 1817-02
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Comprehensive economic, agricultural, demographic, and fiscal survey of the Pacitan Regency submitted by Resident Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst to Governor-General Godert van der Capellen. The report describes Pacitan as an isolated, mountainous coastal valley (requiring ten days' overland transit from the court capitals), ecologically suited for sugar, indigo, pepper, mountain rice, castor oil, and tobacco. Nahuys notes that the recorded population of 22,000 across 122 villages (253 *jungs* of wetland rice fields) was systematically undercounted by indigenous village heads seeking to minimize tax liabilities. The document outlines the pre-1812 dual administration by Yogyakarta and Surakarta under separate demangs; the history of VOC forced pepper deliveries (formerly 300,000 vines delivering 800 pikuls at 3.5 Spanish Dollars per pikul); the collapse of local cloth weaving due to the total export of raw indigo; mountain timber transport inflation (teak selling at 64 stuivers / 3.20 Guilders); rice price volatility (25 stuivers in bumper harvests vs. 1 Spanish Dollar during famine); and identifies poor transport infrastructure as the root cause of provincial impoverishment. The annual net revenue yield totaled 3,289 [Rupees/Dollars] (gross 3,658 minus 369 administrative expenses), exclusive of tollgates and opium farms.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Godert Alexander Gerard Philip van der Capellen [G.G.].
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Pacitan [Pacjitan], Yogyakarta [Djocja], Surakarta [Solo], South Coast (*Segara Kidul*), Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie [VOC], forced pepper deliveries (*pepercultuur*), indigo export monopoly, domestic textile decline, *Jung* [land measure], pikul, *Stuiver* [Dutch coin, 20 to the guilder], Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.], dual administration (*mengelklompen*).

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence, Februari 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Official economic survey, regional monograph, and fiscal assessment submitted to the Governor-General.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the colonial transition from eighteenth-century VOC forced delivery contracts to nineteenth-century bureaucratic exploitation, detailing how peripheral geography, mountainous bottlenecks, and forced cash-crop quotas impoverished remote regencies.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Governor-General Van der Capellen, and indigenous district demangs.
* **Archival Header:** Top registers archival repository notations (`N VAN B / ARCHIVE / LEIDEN / DJOCJA CORR (1817) / (FEB) 1817 (Feb) / REPORT to G.G. ON DJOCJA 1817:`).
* **Topical Headings:** Margin tracks economic commodities (`1 PEPPER:`, `2 INDIGO:`, `TOBACCO:`, `TEAK WOOD:`, `PLENTY of RICE`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Commodities, demographics, and fiscal tallies on the left; verbatim agricultural yields, price variations, infrastructural critiques, and net revenue ledgers on the right.
