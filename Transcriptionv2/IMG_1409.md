# Transcription of IMG_1409.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1409.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Port. 5, No. 11: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence General, May 1817 / Agrarian Reforms & Opium Contraband)
- **Date Range:** 1817-05-01 to 1817-05-04
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic analysis of rural administrative predation, Nahuys's five-point rehabilitation plan for Pacitan, and high-level opium smuggling involving royal court households. The dispatch observes that the extreme tenure insecurity of village officials (*bekels*, *demangs*) drove them into reckless corruption: *"thinks of nothing else. But by corruption and embezzlement of money, and even sometimes participation in kècu parties to make/amass a fortune in as short a time as possible."* To protect peasant producers, Nahuys ordered the public posting of official coffee purchase prices. Confronting the collapse of Pacitan's pepper cultivation (declining from 300,000 to 57,000 vines), Nahuys instituted a comprehensive five-point reform program: (1) planting 50,000 new vines; (2) halving the regency land tax from 3,658 to 1,829; (3) offering peasants the option to pay ground rent in cash or produce after four years; (4) guaranteeing a government purchase price of 3 Spanish Dollars per pikul; and (5) appointing a resident European civil overseer drawn from Yogyakarta burghers. On 4 May, colonial excise agents intercepted an illicit opium ring involving the household of Tumenggung Wirawinata, whose concubine (*selir*) was caught retailing smuggled contraband opium. The dispatch concludes by comparing the fiscal distribution of contraband seizure proceeds between the British and Dutch administrations.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Tumenggung Wirawinata [Tum. Wira Winata], concubine of Wirawinata [Seri-dek / selir], licensed Chinese opium farmer (*pachter*).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Pacitan, Yogyakarta [Djogja], Vorstenlanden, Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bekel*, *Demang*, venality of office, *Kècu* [armed dacoity], coffee price publication, Pacitan pepper reform plan, land tax halving (*landhuur*), contraband opium (*sluikhandel*), seizure prize division rules (British vs. Dutch systems), Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5, No. 11 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence, Mei 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Policy reform programs, agricultural development memoranda, and excise police investigation minutes.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the dual imperatives of early Restoration administration: attempting enlightened agrarian reform (halving peasant taxes to revive cash-crop cultivation) while battling rampant black-market contraband reaching into elite priyayi households.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, excise agents, regional priyayi (Tumenggung Wirawinata), and licensed Chinese opium monopolists.
* **Thematic Section Divisions:** Sheet organized into three thematic sections: (1) Systemic Corruption of Under-Officials, (2) Five-Point Pacitan Agrarian Program, and (3) Opium Smuggling & Seizure Ratios.
* **Numbered Program:** Clear five-point enumeration outlining the Pacitan rehabilitation scheme.
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, policy headings, and legal rubrics on the left; verbatim diagnostic quotes, reform points, smuggling arrests, and British-Dutch comparative ratios on the right.
