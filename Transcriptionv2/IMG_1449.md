# Transcription of IMG_1449.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1449.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Besluit van de Algemeene Secretarie, 8 March 1819, No. 9 / Schneither Collection: Opgave over de Staat der Scholen)
- **Date Range:** 1819-03-08 to 1819-05-24
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Official survey of traditional Islamic education (*pesantrens* and *madrasahs*) across the Residencies of Kedu and Rembang, pursuant to Article 100 of the 1818 Government Regulation (*Reglement op het beleid der Regeering*) and G.J.Chr. Schneither's statistical education audit. On 15 April 1819, Resident of Kedu F.E. Hardy submitted an exhaustive inventory of religious teachers (*kyai*) and traditional Islamic texts (*kitabs*) across Kedu: in Probolinggo (Desa Pabelan and Kaweron / Pesantren Kuweron), Kyai Muhamad, Jagaria, Maspia, and Neda Muhamad taught classical Arabic texts—*Kitab Tasrif* (Arabic morphology), *Kitab Usul* (theology / *usul al-din*), *Kitab Fiqh* (*Pekih* / jurisprudence), and *Tafsir* (*Tapsir* / Qur'anic exegesis)—reading the Arabic text and translating it into vernacular Javanese (*gandhul* interlinear commentary). In District Menoreh (Desa Kedhong Lumpang, Ngalian, Kinangan) and Bandungan (Windusari, Tranem), teachers including Ahmad Isul, Mudangé, Markamah, Bayem Kirawi, Ahmad Nargari, and Ahmad Pekih taught Arabic grammar (*Kitab Nahwu*) and morphology (*Tasrif*). Hardy noted that vernacular Javanese literacy remained virtually nonexistent outside priyayi households, urging the appointment of one secular state teacher per district. On 24 May 1819, Resident of Rembang A.H. Smissaert reported on Islamic education in Rembang and Lasem: 32 teachers in Rembang taught the *Turutan* (Qur'anic reader), while 13 teachers in Lasem instructed children in *Alif-alifan* (the 30 Arabic letters), compensated traditionally by parents with 5 *katies* of rice during Ramadan (*Beras Fitrah*).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, G.J.Chr. Schneither, Resident of Kedu (F.E. Hardy), Resident of Rembang (Anthonië Hendrik Smissaert), Kyai Muhamad (Pabelan), Kyai Maspia (Kaweron / Kuweron), Neda Muhamad (Paré Prapak), Ahmad Isul, Mudangé, Markamah, Bayem Kirawi, Ahmad Pekih (Kedhung Karang, Magelang).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Kedu [Këdhu], Pabelan, Kaweron [Kuweron], Menoreh [Mënorèh], Windusari, Bandungan, Magelang [Magilang], Rembang [Rimbang], Lasem.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Islamic pesantren education, *Kitab Kuning*, *Kitab Nahwu* [syntax], *Kitab Tasrif* [morphology], *Kitab Usul* [theology], *Kitab Fiqh* [jurisprudence], *Tafsir*, *Turutan*, *Alif-alifan*, interlinear Javanese translation (*pegon* / *gandhul*), *Zakat Fitrah* [Beras Fitrah], Reglement Art. 100.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Besluit van de Algemeene Secretarie, 8 March 1819, No. 9; Schneither Collection (Nationaal Archief / KITLV).
* **Document Nature:** Executive statistical returns, ethnographic curriculum surveys, and institutional inventories of Islamic schooling.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Provides an unprecedented empirical map of Central Java’s Islamic intellectual network on the eve of the Java War, identifying key rural ulama and their classical Arabic curricula.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Hardy of Kedu, Resident Smissaert of Rembang, Government Commissioner Schneither, and rural Islamic scholars (*kyai*).
