- **Date Range:** 1817-03-31 to 1817-05-14
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Colonial policy debates over Vorstenlanden demographic censuses and Resident Nahuys van Burgst's landmark strategic analysis of Yogyakarta Kraton politics. In response to the 31 March 1817 order from Finance President Bousquet demanding population censuses, the Commissarissen-Generaal resolved on 14 May 1817 to cancel all demographic surveying in the princely territories. Resident Nahuys (writing from his mountain retreat at Séla on 14 April) warned that sending census commissioners into the countryside would re-ignite deep-seated Javanese terrors of fresh colonial territorial annexations. Yogyakarta court circles still bitterly resented the loss of the rich province of Kedu (annexed by Raffles in 1812) and actively plotted its retrocession. Nahuys provided a searing evaluation of royal factions: Pangéran Paku Alam I was universally loathed by all Yogyakarta princes without exception, detested by the minor Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV for his independent treaty status permitting European ceremonial parity, and remembered with hatred for assisting the British in storming the Kraton in June 1812. Although Paku Alam constantly manufactured intrigues against Ratu Ibu and HB IV (as demonstrated during the Kyai Murma affair), only the dispossessed partisans of the exiled Sultan Hamengkubuwana II (*Kasepuhan*) harbored genuine revanchist ambitions to regain their lands through war; however, Nahuys discounted their threat due to their complete lack of capital, courage, and popular following.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** Nationaal Archief (The Hague), Exhibitum 14 Mei 1817; Exhibitum 1 Mei 1817: Nahuys van Burgst to Commissarissen-Generaal, 14 April 1817 (written from Séla).
* **Document Nature:** High-level political memorandum, intelligence analysis, and executive council minutes on colonial policy in the Principalities (*Vorstenlanden*).
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illuminates how acute geopolitical vulnerabilities—specifically the traumatic memory of Daendels' and Raffles' annexations—dictated colonial non-intervention in rural demographics, while mapping the structural factionalism pitting the Pakualaman against the senior Kraton lines.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, the Commissarissen-Generaal in Batavia, royal dynasts (Paku Alam I, HB IV, Ratu Ibu), and the *Kasepuhan* opposition.
