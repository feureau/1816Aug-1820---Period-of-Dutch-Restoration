- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Part VIII: The Malang & East Java Disturbances / Nationaal Archief, Exhibitum [Exh.] 14 Jul. 1817 No. 1a Secret / ANRI, Djokjo Brieven Bundel 60)
- **Date Range:** 1817-07-03 to 1817-07-14
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Military mobilization and field reconnaissance during the July 1817 Malang and East Java insurrection. Pangéran Dipasana, leading 100 followers equipped with 500 muskets, live cartridges (*scherpe geweerpatronen*), two European sergeants, and two dragoons, marched toward Kediri to secure the border. On 7 July 1817, Acting Resident Von Lippe ordered Sergeant Nolten and cavalry dragoons from the Surakarta Residency Guard to advance to Kediri and coordinate with local Javanese priyayi in capturing the aristocratic ringleaders (Raden Jaladkara, Raden Kramadipura, Raden Kramadiwiria, and Wecadiwiria), who were masquerading as a princely hunting party while secretly plotting to seize Pacitan and Malang. Meanwhile, Pieter Herbert Baron van Lawick van Pabst (Temporary Resident of Surabaya) reported that the band of 40 Solonese insurgents in Malang had retreated to Desa Nuru in the Srengat district, swelling to over 100 men. A field dispatch from Captain Frederick, military commandant of Pasuruan, revealed that trading peasants from Desa Palem encountered 40 insurgents armed with blunderbusses (*donderbussen*), pikes, cutlasses (*klewangs*), and javelins, who claimed to be hunting retainers of Pangéran Prangwedana; following an inquest by the indigenous magistrate (*jaksa*) of Malang confirming plans to storm the district seat, Captain Frederick dispatched a detachment of 30 Javanese soldiers under European non-commissioned officers to assist the Patih of Malang in dispersing the rebels.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** Nationaal Archief (The Hague), Exhibitum 14 Juli 1817 No. 1a Secret; ANRI, Djokjo Brieven Bundel 60; Military Command Correspondence (Oost-Indisch Leger).
* **Document Nature:** Military intelligence reports, field orders, and civil-military coordination minutes dispatched between the garrisons of Pasuruan, Surabaya, Surakarta, and Batavia.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Documents tactical counter-insurgency operations in East Java, revealing how rebel priyayi combined traditional hunting party ruses with modern firearms to threaten colonial outposts across the Kediri-Malang corridor.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Dutch garrison commanders (Capt. Frederick, Sgt. Nolten), colonial Residents (Van Lawick van Pabst, Von Lippe), indigenous magistrates (Patih and Jaksa of Malang), and royal princes (Pangéran Dipasana).
* **Section Heading:** Centered thematic rubric (`Malang Disturbances`).
* **Parallel Operational Notes:** Dual columns tracking field dispatches from Surakarta (Lippe to Nolten) alongside intelligence reports from East Java (Van Pabst to Lippe, Capt. Frederick to Batavia).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Military units, commanders, and dates on the left; tactical troop movements, weapon inventories, rebel pretexts, and magistrate reports on the right.
