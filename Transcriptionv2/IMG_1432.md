# Transcription of IMG_1432.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1432.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5: Nahuys van Burgst Papers / Surakarta Residency Letters, April 1818)
- **Date Range:** 1818-03-20 to 1818-04-30
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Succession intrigues inside the Surakarta Kraton, aristocratic debt and Chinese moneylending, Resident Rijck van Prehn's diplomatic intercession with Sunan Pakubuwana IV, and the formal petition to elevate Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II) to the princely title of *Pangéran Adipati Anom*. On 20 March 1818, Resident Van Prehn reported to Governor-General Van der Capellen regarding rumors that Sunan PB IV intended to disinherit the Crown Prince (Sugandi, future PB V) in favor of his charismatic son Pangéran Purbaya (future PB VII); at a birthday dinner for the Resident, the Sunan firmly denied this, though he expressed deep anxiety over his sons' heavy gambling debts, expressing gratitude that Van Prehn had barred Chinese creditors from entering the Kraton to demand payment from royal purses. In April 1818, Van Prehn submitted a landmark petition to Van der Capellen urging that Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II) be granted the elevated hereditary title of *Pangéran Adipati Anom*. Van Prehn reviewed the history of the Mangkunagaran house: confirmed under Governor Oosting in 1786, Prangwedana had been denied the title of *Adipati* because he was a grandson through the maternal line. The dispatch catalogs the financial subsidies and rewards bestowed on Prangwedana: an annual subsidy of 18,000 Spanish Dollars under Daendels, 38,200 guilders private subsidy from Governor Van Rheede tot Drakenstein, a 20,000 Spanish Dollar prize for storming the Yogyakarta Kraton in June 1812, alongside carriages, hounds, and bloodstock. However, saddled with 36 children, 44 grandchildren, and an armed force of 600 legionnaires requiring 1,200 dollars monthly, Prangwedana faced chronic fiscal insolvency.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Sunan Pakubuwana IV, Crown Prince of Surakarta (future Sunan Pakubuwana V / Sugandi), Pangéran Purbaya (future Sunan Pakubuwana VII), Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II), Resident Rijck van Prehn, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Herman Willem Daendels, Johan Frederik baron van Rheede van de Parkeler tot Drakenstein.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Surakarta [Solo / Sala], Kraton Surakarta, Yogyakarta [Djocja], Batavia, Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Dynastic succession, *Kraton* debts, Chinese creditors barred from palace, *Pangéran Adipati Anom* title petition, Mangkunagaran Legion (600 men under arms), British prize money (1812 Fall of Yogya), royal allowances, princely stipends.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Surakarta Residency Dispatches, April 1818).
* **Document Nature:** Executive diplomatic letters, confidential dynastic intelligence, and financial-military subsidy petitions.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Reveals the complex internal rivalries of the Surakarta dynasty (PB IV, Crown Prince Sugandi, and Purbaya) and documents the Dutch policy of elevating the Mangkunagaran house as a counterweight to the Sunan.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Rijck van Prehn, Governor-General Van der Capellen, Sunan Pakubuwana IV, and Pangéran Prangwedana.
