# Transcription of IMG_1347.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1347.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Baud Collection No. 306: W. H. van IJsseldijk, *Rapport omtrent de Vorstenlanden*, 11 December 1816)
- **Date Range:** 1816-10-22 to 1816-12-11
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic analysis of criminal networks and policing strategy in the *Vorstenlanden*: robbery, cattle rustling, and dacoity plots are routinely organized inside licensed urban opium dens (*opiumkit*) and illicit gambling / pawn venues (*jandakan* / *candhakan*); police directives for bupatis (*Regents*): interrogate frequenters of opium dens, taking advantage of the psychological fact that under the disinhibiting influence of opium, Javanese suspects will confess freely and betray their accomplices; criminal investigations into highway robberies and murders: defenseless Chinese traveling merchants and peddlers are identified as the primary victims of deadly highway assaults; institutional revival of the traditional collective highway homicide fine (*denda pati* / *tanggung-renteng*): an indemnity fine of 200 Spanish Dollars (Sp. D.) levied collectively upon surrounding villages whenever a murdered corpse is discovered on a public roadway; apportionment formula: the village nearest the corpse pays half (100 Sp. D.), while outlying villages pay diminishing proportional shares; urban crime links: rural bandits and perpetrators routinely shelter in town opium dens; restitution clause: if the murderer is apprehended within 40 days, the 200 Sp. D. fine is fully refunded to the villagers; colonial precedent: Van IJsseldijk notes that this collective indemnity system proved extraordinarily effective during his own tenure as Dutch Resident of Yogyakarta (1786–1798); root causes of agrarian criminality: corrupt junior officials (*bekel* / tax-collectors) extort up to three times (3x) the legitimate tax quota from the peasantry; peasant ruin: cultivators stripped of livestock and harvest abandon their lands to lead a desperate, roving bandit existence (*roving existence*).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Wouter Hendrik van IJsseldijk.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta (Yogya), Surakarta, Central Java.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Opium dens (*opiumkit*), gambling / pawn joints (*jandakan* / *candhakan*), bupati policing duties, highway robbery, Chinese peddlers / traders, collective murder fine (200 Sp. D. / *denda pati*), collective liability (*tanggung-renteng*), 40-day investigative limit, 1786–1798 residency precedent, threefold tax extortion, peasant dispossession, rural banditry (*kècu*).
