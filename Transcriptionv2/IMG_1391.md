## Metadata

- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (KITLV H 698b: AvJ 1817 [Yogya], March–July 1817 / Nahuys Dispatches to Commissarissen-Generaal & J.C. Baud)
- **Date Range:** 1817-03-14 to 1817-07-05
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Governance of princely apanages, royal pensions for exiled families, border village partitions, and strategic counter-insurgency doctrine in Yogyakarta. A top fiscal balance compares net revenues between the Sultan’s and Sunan’s shared territories (yielding 1,986 and 1,672 Spanish Dollars respectively, producing a net profit of 3,289 after deducting 369 Rupees administrative expenses, exclusive of tollgates and opium monopolies). On 14 March 1817, Resident Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst reported to the Commissarissen-Generaal that Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV had undertaken the maintenance of the abandoned families of exiled princes: Ratu Mas (consort of Pangéran Mangkudiningrat I) received 50 *cacah* and 50 Spanish Dollars annuity; Raden Tumenggung Mangkuwijaya received 350 *cacah*; and Raden Ayu Juruanenggorn (mother of Pangéran Mertasana) received 8 *cacah*. On 5 April, Nahuys clarified the dual jurisdictional partition of Desa Klepu / Klaten (administered equally by Yogyakarta and Surakarta). In a second dispatch of 5 April, Nahuys evaluated princely unrest: Pangéran Paku Alam I was judged "too fearful to attempt anything against the Government," still harboring hopes of being appointed Sultan; while the impoverished *Kasepuhan* princes loyal to the exiled Sultan HB II, having lost their apanages and Kedu revenues, were orchestrating attacks on tollgates (*bandar*). To suppress this, Nahuys reiterated his policy of stationing three soldiers at every tollgate and encouraging private European agricultural settlement to stimulate indigenous industry and enhance island defense.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** KITLV H 698b; transcripts of Yogyakarta Residency Dispatches (Maart–Juli 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Administrative correspondence, pension rolls, border arbitration notices, and high-level political memoranda.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates how the royal court contained dynastic destitution among exiles' dependents, arbitrated overlapping territorial enclaves (*enclaves en mengelklompen*), and combated guerrilla strikes on fiscal tollgates organized by dispossessed *Kasepuhan* princes.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Algemene Secretarie official J.C. Baud, Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, and royal dependents (Ratu Mas, Mangkuwijaya).
* **Archival Header:** Top line registers archival code and series (`H 698b. / AvJ. / 1817 - 1817 - March / July / (Yogya)`).
* **Chronological Ledger:** Left column tracks exact dispatch dates (`14 Mar`, `5 April`, `5 April`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, addressees, and fiscal headings on the left; verbatim apanage tallies, dynastic pensions, political evaluations, and tollgate defense schemes on the right.
