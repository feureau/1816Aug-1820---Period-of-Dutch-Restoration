# Transcription of IMG_1453.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1453.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Kabinet Dossier No. 2065, 31 Dec. 1832: Local Education Inquest 1831 / Reports from Semarang, Kedu, and Bagelèn)
- **Date Range:** 1831-09-25 to 1832-12-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Official proposals for secular regency primary schools, aristocratic status symbols for native schoolmasters, containment of Islamic pilgrimage (*Haji*) influence in Kedu, and a historical survey of the royal *perdikan* pesantren of Alang-Alang Ombo in Bagelèn. On 24 December 1831, General Secretary P. le Clercq recommended establishing at least one state primary school in every regency, proposing monthly salaries of f. 40 in Semarang and f. 30 in Kendal and Demak, endowing schoolmasters with the official priyayi rank of *Demang* and authorizing them to carry a state umbrella (*payung*) as insignia of royal-colonial dignity. On 25 September 1831, the Secretary of Kedu reported five primary schools in operation, emphasizing that establishing secular government schools was vital to neutralize the anti-colonial political influence of Mecca pilgrims (*Hajis*) and rural priests; the report demanded that schoolbooks strictly omit both Christian dogmas (rejecting the *Bijbelgenootschap*) and Islamic theological doctrines, proposing a three-tier faculty (Javanese script, Arabic texts, and Roman script/arithmetic) overseen by a local commission comprising the Resident, Regent, Head Pengulu, and Head Jaksa. On 20 October 1831, Resident of Bagelèn J.H.W. Smissaert reported twenty village religious schools, highlighting the premier regional academy: **Pesantren Alang-Alang Ombo** directed by Kyai Guru Kastuba [Kutubo], an ancient tax-free religious freehold (*perdikan*) originally granted by the Yogyakarta Kraton and confirmed under Dutch rule, educating high-born priyayi sons.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Johannes van den Bosch, P. le Clercq, Resident of Kedu, J.H.W. Smissaert (Resident of Bagelèn), Kyai Guru Kastuba (Alang-Alang Ombo), D.W. van Lawick van Pabst, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, District Regents, Head Pengulus, Head Jaksas.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Semarang, Kendal, Demak, Kedu [Këdhu], Bagelèn [Bagëlen], Alang-Alang Ombo, Yogyakarta, Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Regency primary schools (*Inlandsche Scholen*), Demang rank for teachers, State umbrella (*payung*), Hajis' political influence, strict secular neutrality, *Jaksa* [indigenous magistrate], *Pesantren Alang-Alang Ombo*, *Perdikan* [tax-exempt royal domain], *Bijbelgenootschap*.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Kabinet Dossier No. 2065 (Resoluties van de Gouverneur-Generaal in Rade, December 1832).
* **Document Nature:** Executive administrative proposals, security evaluations of Islamic influence, and cadastral reports on religious freeholds (*perdikan*).
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates colonial post-war pacification strategy: attempting to institutionalize secular public education under strict priyayi-judicial oversight while monitoring independent rural religious redoubts like Alang-Alang Ombo.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Governor-General Van den Bosch, General Secretary P. le Clercq, Resident Smissaert of Bagelèn, and regional regency boards.
