# Transcription of IMG_1390.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1390.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (KITLV H 698b: Jun–Oct 1817 / Yogyakarta & Surakarta Residency Dispatches, Besluiten van den Gouverneur-Generaal)
- **Date Range:** 1817-02-04 to 1817-10-15
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Critical intelligence, viceregal itineraries, tollgate fiscal statistics, and the agricultural decline of Pacitan during 1817. On 8 June, Surakarta Assistant Resident Von Lippe warned Resident Nahuys that three Indian Sepoys intercepted under police escort possessed Batavian travel permits but were suspected of treasonous liaison with Pangéran Mangkubumi of Surakarta, having deserted from the Bengal Volunteer battalion that orchestrated the 1815 Surakarta conspiracy. On 7 and 11 July, decrees regulated the Commissarissen-Generaal's grand inspection tour through Central Java (August–September 1817) and confirmed Pangéran Dipakusuma as acting Bupati Wedana of Madiun and the eastern *Manca Nagara*. On 15 October, Resident Valck of Kedu reported the arson destruction of Bandar Krètèg (*rangkah* customs post belonging to the Sultan of Yogyakarta). A retrospective statistical return of 4 February reveals the dramatic escalation in annual Yogyakarta tollgate farming revenues under British administration: surging from 46,557 Spanish Dollars in 1812 to 147,818 Spanish Dollars by 1815. Finally, an economic survey of Pacitan (22,000 inhabitants, 253 *jungs*, 122 villages) documents the collapse of forced pepper cultivation: compulsory planting over 25 years caused vines to plunge from 300,000 to 37,000, yielding a meager 800 pikuls delivered to the colonial monopoly at 3.5 Spanish Dollars per pikul.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** KITLV H 698b; transcripts of official dispatches and statistical returns from the Residency of Yogyakarta (1817).
* **Document Nature:** Police intelligence notices, viceregal itineraries, fiscal toll-farm returns, and regional economic surveys.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the hyper-monetization of the Javanese interior under British and early Restoration rule (tripling of tollgate values), the environmental and demographic exhaustion of forced cash-crop schemes (Pacitan pepper collapse), and continued colonial vigilance regarding ex-Sepoy mercenaries.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Residents Nahuys, Von Lippe, and Valck; the Commissarissen-Generaal; regional regents (Dipakusuma); and Bengal Sepoy deserters.
* **Continuation Ledger:** Direct sequence continuing KITLV H 698b notes from `IMG_1389.jpg`.
* **Topical Marginalia:** Left margin tracks archival entries and agricultural trends (`Decline pepper gardens`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dispatch dates and administrative sources on the left; verbatim surveillance notices, inspection itineraries, tollgate revenue figures, and pepper production statistics on the right.
