# Transcription of IMG_1408.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1408.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Port. 5, No. 11: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence General, May 1817 / Djogja to Governor-General)
- **Date Range:** 1816-08 to 1817-05-01
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Commercial bidding for the colonial opium farm monopoly in Yogyakarta, retail distribution mechanisms, priyayi impoverishment, and the venality of village offices. On 1 May 1817, Lieutenant-Titular of the Chinese Si Tanju [Si Tonjoe] submitted a formal tender to acquire the Yogyakarta opium monopoly farm, offering an advance premium of 600 Spanish Dollars and guaranteeing 1,200 Spanish Dollars per chest of raw opium (agreeing to meet any future state price escalations). The existing opium farm quota stood at 40 chests annually for Surakarta and 40 chests for Yogyakarta, leased to Lim Hoek Sing at 1,200 Spanish Dollars per chest (recommended to be raised to 1,500 Spanish Dollars). Nahuys endorsed Si Tonjoe as an experienced local merchant uniquely skilled at suppressing illicit contraband. In a concurrent report of 1 May, Nahuys lamented the decay of coffee and pepper cultivation in Yogyakarta and Pacitan, observing the alarming poverty of junior princes and priyayi (*"There are so many poor Princes and Priyayi here that it is in the best interests of both they and the government that something should be dedicated to it"*), and condemning the venal auction of village offices (*bekel*, *demang*, *lurah*) to the highest cash bidder, citing the case of the dismissed Demang of Boyolali where rival applicants tendered 500 Spanish Dollar bribes.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Si Tanju / Si Tonjoe [Lieut. Titular of Chinese], Lim Hoek Sing [opium farmer], Demang of Boyolali, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip van der Capellen [G.G.].
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Pacitan, Boyolali, Vorstenlanden [VK], Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Opium farm monopoly (*opiumpacht*), Chinese Officers (*Luitenant der Chinezen*), chests of raw opium, contraband smuggling, venality of office (*ambtsverkoop*), *Bekel*, *Demang*, *Lurah*, coffee and pepper plantations, Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5, No. 11 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence, Mei 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Commercial monopoly tenders, revenue farm evaluations, and socio-economic diagnostic reports submitted to the Governor-General.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Reveals the financial centrality of the Chinese-run opium farm in funding the colonial state, while linking the structural corruption of the indigenous village administration (bribes for headmanships) to the economic destitution of the court aristocracy.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Chinese officers (Si Tonjoe, Lim Hoek Sing), Governor-General Van der Capellen, and rural priyayi officials.
* **Archival Header:** Top registers repository and dispatch markers (`BPL 616 / NvB v.B / PORT 5 / NO. 11. / DJOCJA CORR (GENERAL) / (MAY 1817) / Djogja -> G.G. / 1817 [May] / N van B ARCHIVE LEIDEN`).
* **Thematic Sections:** Margin tracks monopoly tenders (`1st May 1817 Request of Lieut. Titular of Chinese di Tanju`, `PRESENT VORK of OPIUM MONOPOLY:`, `POOR STATE of COFFEE / PEPPER PLANTATIONS`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, tender terms, and official titles on the left; verbatim bidding amounts, quotas, priyayi poverty quotes, and Boyolali corruption details on the right.
