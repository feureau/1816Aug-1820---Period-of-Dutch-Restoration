# Transcription of IMG_1417.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1417.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Portfolio 5, No. 11)
- **Date Range:** 1817-09 to 1817-10
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Philosophical debate between Resident Nahuys van Burgst and the colonial Council of Finances (*Raad van Financiën*) over private European land leasing (*landhuur*) in the *Vorstenlanden*, alongside specific terms for Carl Coster's 10-year lease in Pacitan. Nahuys articulated the principle of laissez-faire agrarian contracts: *"Ik zoude echter oordeel zyn Dat de Resident in die Vorsten Landen het geheel aan de vrije Keuze van de eigenaars des grond zoude moeten overlaten"* (I would judge that the Resident in those Principalities should leave the matter entirely to the free choice of the owners of the soil), provided the Resident verified that lease contracts with royal princes were equitable and transparent. In response to conservative warnings voiced by Financial Board member S. de Graaff—who argued that granting European planters a free hand would impose a crushing "new yoke" on the Javanese peasantry, far worse than traditional priyayi corruption, leading inevitably to peasant impoverishment—Nahuys countered that while forced deliveries (*contingenten*) in autonomous princely domains inevitably fostered despotic exploitation, free-market plantation agriculture (coffee and pepper) with guaranteed fixed crop prices, cash wages for day-laborers, and contractual tenure for *bekels* and *demangs* was the only path to rural prosperity. Applying these principles, Nahuys endorsed the application of merchant Carl Coster (C.H. Coster) to lease agricultural land in Pacitan for 10 years, with the condition that all physical infrastructure and improvements revert to the government at the end of the term.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, S. de Graaff, Carl Coster [C.H. Coster], Members of the Council of Finances.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Pacitan [Patjitan], Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Landhuur* [private land leasing], *Raad van Financiën* [Council of Finances], *contingenten* [forced deliveries], paternalistic control, commercial coffee and pepper plantations, *bekel* [village headman / tax collector], *demang*, 10-year land concession.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 5, No. 11 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Collection, Djokjakarta Letters and Memoranda, September–October 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Policy exchange, ideological critique of colonial agrarian governance, and contractual lease specifications.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the sharp doctrinal clash between Batavia’s conservative bureaucratic paternalism (fearing capitalist exploitation of the peasantry) and Nahuys’ aggressive liberal-physiocratic vision of European private enterprise in Java.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, colonial financial councillor S. de Graaff, and private entrepreneur C.H. Coster.
* **Archival Header:** Top entries establish the administrative policy query (`RESIDENT to give CHOICE of METHOD even to Western Planters / [red ink: (Sept - Oct?) (1817)]`).
* **Marginal Annotations:** Red ink marginal markers identifying the bureaucratic opposition (`[red ink: cf. de Graaff.]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Policy principles, direct Dutch quotations, and bureaucratic counter-arguments on the left; substantive ideological defenses of free labor, agrarian commercialization, and the Pacitan lease conditions on the right.
