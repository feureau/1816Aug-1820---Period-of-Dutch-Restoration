# Transcription of IMG_1447.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1447.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Besluit van de Algemeene Secretarie, 8 March 1819, No. 9 / Ingekomen Berigten over het Inlandsch Onderwijs)
- **Date Range:** 1819-03-08 to 1819-04-29
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Comprehensive colonial administrative inquest into the condition of native primary education, literacy rates, and Islamic schooling across Java, pursuant to Governor-General Van der Capellen's decree of 8 March 1819 (No. 9). In Pasuruan (19 April 1819), the Resident reported that compulsory government decrees were necessary to enforce school attendance, as peasant parents retained children at home for agricultural chores and buffalo grazing (*kêbo*). In Besuki (26 March), Islamic instruction was managed by district *penghulus* for children aged 5–6; while the *penghulu* accepted pupils without regard to wealth, instruction was confined to rote Arabic recitation, with vernacular Javanese literacy taught exclusively inside elite priyayi households; the Resident proposed appointing one secular Javanese teacher per regency, endowed with 1 *bouw* of land and f. 5 monthly. In Banten (19 March), Resident Van de Wijck revealed extreme illiteracy: out of 800 native overseers (*mandurs*), barely 100 could read or write. In Cianjur / Priangan (24 March), education was conducted by private Sundanese tutors (*guru*) whom pupils served as domestic retainers in lieu of fees; the Resident advocated state-funded schools in every village for reading and writing Javanese, with district capital academies teaching Malay and Jawi (Arabic script) to prepare sons of officials for colonial service. In Tegal (29 April), Resident J.W. Meilan reported that during cadastral land rent surveys, barely 3 to 4 out of every 50 village heads (*bekels* / *lurah*) could write their own names.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Resident of Pasuruan, Resident of Besuki, Resident of Banten (Van de Wijck), Resident of Cianjur, Resident of Tegal (J.W. Meilan), District Penghulus, Islamic village modins and gurus.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Pasuruan [Pasoeroean], Besuki [Besoeki], Banten, Serang, Tangerang, Priangan [Preanger], Cianjur, Tegal.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Colonial native education inquiry (*Inlandsch Onderwijs*), compulsory schooling, child labor (buffalo herding), *Penghulu* [chief mosque official], *Modin*, *Guru*, Javanese vs. Sundanese literacy, cadastral land surveys, village headmen illiteracy (*bekel* / *lurah*).

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Besluit van de Algemeene Secretarie, 8 March 1819, No. 9 (Reports on the State of Native Schools in Java).
* **Document Nature:** Administrative intelligence reports, ethnographic education surveys, and official policy recommendations.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Documents Batavia’s pioneering 1819 survey of indigenous literacy, exposing the stark reality of rural educational neglect and laying the intellectual foundations for early colonial primary schooling.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Governor-General Van der Capellen, Residents of Pasuruan, Besuki, Banten, Cianjur, and Tegal.
* **Archival Header:** Top entries establish archival series and decree classification (`Local Education 1819 / [Top right:] AN Besluit Alg. Secr. 8 Mar. 1819 no. 9 / BGG 3 March 1819 no. 9 / Local Education`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Regional residency headings and dates on the left; verbatim summaries of educational conditions, literacy statistics, and proposed school systems on the right.
