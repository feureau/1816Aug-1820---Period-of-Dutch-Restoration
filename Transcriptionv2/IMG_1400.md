# Transcription of IMG_1400.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1400.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Economic & Police Reports, Feb 1817 / Comparative Statistical Returns)
- **Date Range:** 1812 to 1817-02
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic colonial critique of gambling regulation, the predatory dynamics of the internal tollgate tax-farming system (*bandar*), and geographical survey data for the directly administered regency of Pacitan. Following personal remonstrances by Governor-General Van der Capellen to Patih Raden Adipati Danureja IV, judicial administration showed slight improvements; however, Resident Nahuys refused to prohibit gambling and games of chance, arguing that a total ban was impossible to enforce and would merely expose the glaring weakness of the municipal police. In a landmark economic critique, Nahuys analyzed the annual auction returns of Yogyakarta tollgates from 1812 to 1816 (citing comparative figures compiled by Hendrik Mauritz MacGillavry), which skyrocketed from 46,557 Spanish Dollars in 1812 to 147,000 Spanish Dollars in 1816. Nahuys demolished the official colonial orthodoxy that rising tollgate revenues proved expanding commercial prosperity, proving instead that they reflected the unbridled extortion of Chinese tax-farmers who squeezed exorbitant rates from impoverished Javanese peasants with the tacit encouragement of colonial Residents seeking maximized auction yields. Finally, a geographic profile records that Pacitan, annexed in August 1812 and administered directly by Europeans, lay 90 miles southeast of Yogyakarta, extending 40 miles along the southern Indian Ocean coast and ranging from 20 to 40 miles in width.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Raden Adipati Danureja IV [Patih], Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Godert Alexander Gerard Philip van der Capellen [Governor-General], Hendrik Mauritz MacGillavry, Chinese tollgate keepers (*bandars*).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djocja], Pacitan [Pachitan], South Coast (*Segara Kidul* / Indian Ocean), Batavia, Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bandar* [customs tollgate], opium farm, gambling houses (*candhakan*), tollgate farming auctions (*verpachting*), predatory fiscal extortion, comparative toll revenue tables (1812–1816), direct European colonial rule, Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Economic and Police Memoranda, Februari 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Administrative policy assessments, statistical time-series analyses, economic critiques of colonial fiscal policy, and geographic regency surveys.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Serves as one of the most powerful contemporary European indictments of the tollgate tax-farming system, providing empirical documentation of the fiscal mechanisms that impoverished the Javanese peasantry and alienated rural society prior to the Java War.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Governor-General Van der Capellen, Acting Resident H.M. MacGillavry, Grand Vizier Danureja IV, and Chinese tax-farming syndicates.
* **Thematic Section Divisions:** Sheet organized into three numbered analytical sections: (1) Police & Gambling, (2) Toll-gates & Bandars (Opium Value), and (3) Pacitan Regency.
* **Comparative Revenue Table:** Clear tabular presentation tracking annual tollgate farm yields from 1812 to 1816.
* **Two-Column Organization:** Analytical headings, dates, and administrative rubrics on the left; verbatim policy reflections, statistical series, economic critiques, and geographic dimensions on the right.
