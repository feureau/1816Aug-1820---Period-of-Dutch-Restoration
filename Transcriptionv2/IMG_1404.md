# Transcription of IMG_1404.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1404.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence General, March–April 1817: Royal Exiles & Highway Staging Posts)
- **Date Range:** 1817-03-04 to 1817-04-05
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Petitions for exile accompaniment by royal consorts, political threat assessments of exiled princes, royal visits to the residency, and a strategic survey of highways and posting stations between Yogyakarta and Surakarta. On 4 March 1817, the *Kapitan Cina* (Tan Jin Sing / Raden Tumenggung Secadiningrat) is noted providing assistance to compromised European officials. On 11 March, three royal ladies of Sultan Sepuh's household—Raden Ayu Murtiningrat, Raden Ayu Dewaningrat, and Raden Ayu Dasiningrat—petitioned for permission to join the exiled monarch in Ambon. On 14 March, maintenance allowances were assigned to the families of Pangéran Arya Mangkudiningrat I (350 *cacah*) and Pangéran Mertasana (25 *cacah*); Nahuys warned that Mangkudiningrat was so charismatic that if repatriated, he would immediately assume the leadership of the anti-Dutch court opposition, while Mertasana was characterized as a selfish, untrustworthy intriguer whose banishment was essential. Highlighting intimate court diplomacy, the young Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV personally visited Nahuys during a period of feigned illness. Finally, an inquiry of 5 April surveyed the post-road between Yogyakarta and Surakarta, documenting the divided jurisdiction of Desa Klepu, overnight posting accommodations at Boyolali (including the country estate of Mr. de la Bretonnière at Valheygat), and the sequence of intermediate tollgates at Prambanan Lor, Klaten, and Baki.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Raden Tumenggung Secadiningrat / Tan Jin Sing [Capitan Tjijna], Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV [HB IV / Sultan], Sultan Hamengkubuwana II [HB II / Old Sultan], Pangéran Arya Mangkudiningrat I, Pangéran Arya Mertasana, Raden Ayu Murtiningrat, Raden Ayu Dewaningrat, Raden Ayu Dasiningrat, Mr. de la Bretonnière.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Boyolali, Desa Klepu [Kleppu], Prambanan Lor, Klaten [Klatten], Baki [Bakkio], Valheygat, Ambon / Moluccas, Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Kapitan Cina*, royal concubines / princesses (*Radèn Ayu*), exile to Ambon, *Cacah* [appanage units], post-roads (*postwegen*), staging stations (*pasanggrahan*), tollgate barriers (*bandar*), divided villages (*mengelklompen*).

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence, Maart–April 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Diplomatic diaries, royal petitions, political security dossiers, and transportation infrastructure surveys.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Chronicles the diplomatic consolidation of Dutch authority: managing the departure of Sultan Sepuh's entourage, neutralizing princely revanchism through permanent exile, and mapping the strategic road and toll network connecting the two royal capitals.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Kapitan Cina Tan Jin Sing, Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, royal consorts, and European estate owners (De la Bretonnière).
* **Chronological Ledger:** Left column marks exact dates spanning March and April 1817 (`4 Mrt.`, `11 Mrt.`, `14 Mrt`, `5 April`).
* **Section Rubrics:** Margin tracks highway milestones (`INQUIRY into STATIEG highways & POSTING STATIONS`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, royal names, and transit stations on the left; verbatim petitions, character evaluations, court diplomatic visits, and road itineraries on the right.
