## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1380.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Arsip Nasional Republik Indonesia [ANRI], Solo Brieven Bundel 131: 1818 / *Translaaten en Verbalen van den jaar 1816 tot 1818* [Surakarta Court Reports])
- **Date Range:** 1818-02-04
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic documentation of rural village literacy, tax defaults, and riverine trade contraband along the Bengawan Solo waterway. The first judicial entry records the dismissal and property confiscation of Bekel Risawidjaya of Keong Awi by Demang Mas Nitikrama on 26 Sura 1744 [c. November 1816] for default on village tax remittances (*uang pajak*); his seized personal inventory provides rare, quantitative evidence of rural literacy and religious learning, containing a handwritten Qur'an valued at 5 Spanish Dollars, an Islamic theological treatise (*kitab*) valued at 1 Spanish Dollar, and an astrological/divinatory handbook (*primbon*) valued at 0.25 Spanish Dollars. The second record consists of a formal memorandum (*nota*) dated 4 February 1818 by Surakarta Residency Translator Johannes Wilhelmus Winter documenting an illicit riverine commercial expedition led by Raden Dipawijaya; departing Surakarta on 11 October with three large river craft (*prahu*) laden with 13 *koyang* of rice and 10 *koyang* of indigo bound for the coastal port of Gresik, Dipawijaya evaded mandatory transit tolls at seven river toll stations (*bandar*). His return cargo comprised 4,000 blocks of fermented shrimp paste (*trasi*), 60 *koyang* of ginger (*gember*), 3 *koyang* of maize (*jagung*), 1,000 Chinese porcelain plates, 1 *koyang* of salt, and fine textiles (*afstoffen*), evading the standard toll levy of 7 *dubbeltjes* per boat at each tollgate.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Bekel Risawidjaya, Demang Mas Nitikrama, Johannes Wilhelmus Winter [J.W. Winter], Raden Dipawijaya, Inas Sampurna.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Surakarta [Solo], Keong Awi, Bengawan Solo [River Solo], Gresik [Grisik], North Coast (*Pasisir*).
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bekel* [village tax collector], *Demang* [district head], *Uang Pajak* [cash tax], Qur'an, *Kitab* [Islamic religious text], *Primbon* [divinatory compendium], rural literacy, Bengawan Solo riverine trade, *Bandar* [river tollgate / customs house], *Prahu* [river boats], *Koyang* [measure of weight/capacity approx. 27–30 pikuls / c. 1.8 metric tons], *Trasi* [fermented shrimp paste], Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.], *Dubbeltje* [Dutch two-stiver coin / 10 cents].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI, Solo Brieven Bundel 131 (1818), containing the official bound compilation titled *Translaaten en Verbalen van den jaar 1816 tot 1818*.
* **Document Nature:** Official Dutch translations of Surakarta regional court records (*Verbalen van het Hof van Solo*) and commercial inquests drafted by Residency Translator J.W. Winter.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illuminates two crucial socio-economic phenomena of late traditional Java: (1) the surprising depth of Islamic scriptural literacy and textual ownership among village-level headmen (*bekels*), and (2) the extensive, highly monetized bulk commodity trade flowing along the Bengawan Solo between the royal interior (*Vorstenlanden*) and the north coast ports (*Pasisir*).
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Surakarta court translator J.W. Winter, regional demangs and bekels, and indigenous aristocratic merchant-traders (Raden Dipawijaya).
* **Archival Header:** Top line records archival volume and year (`Solo Br. 131 / 1818`), followed by manuscript series title (`'Translaaten en Verbalen van den jaar 1816 tot 1818' (Court Reports Solo)`).
* **Carey Annotations:** Right margin annotation in Carey's hand emphasizing cultural literacy (`[in margin: on Literacy]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Case entries, legal charges, and dates on the left; property inventories, book valuations, commodity cargoes, and toll rate structures on the right.
