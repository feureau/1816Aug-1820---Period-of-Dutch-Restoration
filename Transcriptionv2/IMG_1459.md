# Transcription of IMG_1459.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1459.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Scholarly Research Notes (Historiographical Excerpt: J.A. van der Chijs, *Tijdschrift voor Indische Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* [TBG], 1864)
- **Date Range:** 1831-09-19 to 1864-12-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Scholarly analytical note by Peter Carey evaluating the administrative outcome of Governor-General Johannes van den Bosch's 1831 education inquest, citing J.A. van der Chijs' seminal study *"Bijdragen tot de geschiedenis van het Inlandsch onderwijs in Nederlandsch-Indië aan officiële bronnen ontleend"* (*TBG*, Vol. 14, 1864, pp. 212–323). Carey highlights Van der Chijs' incisive analysis on pages 215–220 concerning the central agrarian conundrum of colonial schooling: how to harmonize the education of native children with the daily agricultural demands of the village economy (*"onderwijs der kinderen in verband te brengen met de dorpshuishouding?"*). Carey notes that despite the voluminous, detailed statistical and sociological returns submitted by Residents across Java in response to the Cabinet Circular of 19 September 1831 (No. 1431), pages 227–234 of Van der Chijs' treatise reveal the ultimate administrative paralysis of the colonial state: **no concrete governmental action was taken upon the information received**, leaving indigenous schooling to languish without state resources for another two decades until the reforms of the 1850s.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Jacobus Anne van der Chijs (colonial archivist/historian), Johannes van den Bosch, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Dr. Peter B.R. Carey.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Batavia, The Hague.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Tijdschrift voor Indische Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* (TBG), Cabinet Circular No. 1431, Village domestic economy (*dorpshuishouding*), administrative inaction, colonial educational policy, historiographical synthesis.
