## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1465.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Djokjo Brieven Bundel 79: Register op de Aankomende Stukken, January–August 1819)
- **Date Range:** 1819-01-16 to 1819-08-24
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Transfer of supreme executive colonial authority to Governor-General Van der Capellen, administrative appointments in Pacitan, police surveillance of Islamic pilgrims (*Hajis*) crossing provincial borders, food security measures, and the landmark abolition of internal customs tollgates on the Bengawan Solo. On 16 January 1819, following the departure of Commissioners-General Elout and Buyskes, supreme executive authority across the Netherlands Indies was vested solely in Governor-General Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen (formally gazetted in the *Javasche Courant*). On 25 February 1819, C.F. Engel was commissioned as colonial overseer (*opzichter*) of Pacitan to manage agrarian development. On 22 March 1819, the Resident of Kedu warned Resident Nahuys that three notable Islamic religious figures—Haji Abdulrahman, Haji Burhan, and a teacher designated as "Muslim"—had departed Kedu en route to Yogyakarta and Banyumas, triggering border surveillance. On 24 August, the Director-General of Finances instructed Nahuys to secure strategic rice and paddy reserves to avert famine before the next monsoon harvest. Most significantly, on 20 August 1819, Governor-General Van der Capellen enacted an imperial decree **abolishing all internal customs tolls along the entire course of the Bengawan Solo** (*Abolition of tolls Solo R.*), striking down the predatory river transit barriers that had throttled inland commerce for decades.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, C.F. Engel (Opzichter of Pacitan), Resident of Kedu, Haji Abdulrahman, Haji Burhan, Hoofd Directeur van Financiën, Kyai Maja (marginal note).
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djocja], Kedu [Kědhu], Banyumas, Pacitan, Bengawan Solo [Solo River], Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Dissolution of Commissarissen-Generaal, *Javasche Courant*, *Opzichter*, Haji surveillance, food grain reserves (padi / rice), Abolition of Bengawan Solo river tolls, *Kyai Maja*.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Djokjo Brieven Bundel 79 (Register der Ingekomen Stukken van de Residentie Jogjakarta, 1819).
* **Document Nature:** Executive incoming correspondence register, police movement notices, and imperial economic reform decrees.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Captures the commencement of Van der Capellen’s personal rule in 1819 and records his first major liberal reform in the *Vorstenlanden*: abolishing river tolls on Java's greatest waterway.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Governor-General Van der Capellen, Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Kedu Residency officials, and colonial financial directors.
* **Archival Header:** Top entries establish archival bundle classification (`1819 / Jan. - Aug. 1819 / Dj. Br. 79. / Register op de aankomende stukken 1819. / [Van der Cap. takes over.]`).
* **Marginal Annotations:** Sectional markers highlighting executive transitions, toll abolition, and religious leaders (`[Abolition of tolls Solo R.]`, `[Faint red note below: Kyai Maja]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Chronological dispatch dates and correspondents on the left; substantive summaries of supreme power transfer, Pacitan administration, Haji surveillance, grain reserves, and river toll abolition on the right.
