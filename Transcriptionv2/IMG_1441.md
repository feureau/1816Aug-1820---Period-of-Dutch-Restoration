# Transcription of IMG_1441.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1441.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5: Secret Letters from Solo / Van Prehn to Algemeen Secretaris, 30 March 1818, Folio 5)
- **Date Range:** 1818-03-30
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Fifth folio of Resident Rijck van Prehn’s Secret Dispatch No. 2 detailing Crown Prince Sugandi’s punitive disciplinary measures over royal princes, Sunan Pakubuwana IV’s gratitude for colonial regulations curbing Chinese credit to court youths, and a psychological assessment contrasting the Crown Prince’s strictness with his father’s historic cruelty. Van Prehn records that princes who violated palace curfew were sentenced to punitive arrest for one to one-and-a-half months. Sunan PB IV explicitly affirmed to Van Prehn that keeping his royal children firmly under control (*onder de duim*) was an absolute necessity, warmly thanking the Resident for issuing a residency decree capping credit extended by Chinese merchants to 25 Spanish Dollars and prohibiting unauthorized commercial trading inside the palace, which had previously forced the Sunan to liquidate personal assets to pay his children’s debts. Van Prehn reassures Batavia that palace hatred against the Crown Prince was strictly confined to Pangéran Purbaya's maternal faction, refuting claims of general unpopularity. Significantly, Van Prehn notes that while Sugandi was rigorous in discipline, he was entirely devoid of the cruel, tyrannical nature (*wreede inborst*) of Sunan PB IV, whose past reign had claimed numerous innocent victims (such as the assassinations during the Pakepung crisis of 1788–1790).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Resident Rijck van Prehn, Sunan Pakubuwana IV, Crown Prince of Surakarta (Sugandi / future Sunan Pakubuwana V), Pangéran Purbaya (future Sunan Pakubuwana VII), Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Chinese merchants and creditors.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Surakarta [Soerakarta], Kraton Surakarta, Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Princely arrest (1–1½ months), Chinese credit cap (25 Spanish Dollars), palace trade ban, royal indebtedness, psychological profile of PB IV vs. PB V, *wreede inborst* [cruel disposition], Pakepung crisis legacy.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Secret Letters from Solo, 1818, Folio 5).
* **Document Nature:** Secret executive dispatch (*Geheime Missive*), verbatim diplomatic reportage, and royal psychological evaluation.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the institutionalization of colonial fiscal controls over royal priyayi spending and provides rare contemporary insights into the character transition between Sunan PB IV and the future Sunan PB V.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Rijck van Prehn, Sunan Pakubuwana IV, Crown Prince Sugandi, and Chinese merchant guilds.
* **Archival Header:** Continuation of primary secret dispatch text from `IMG_1440`.
* **Marginal Annotations:** Analytical markers identifying royal arrest procedures, credit caps, and character assessments.
* **Two-Column Organization:** Thematic context and regulatory points on the left; verbatim nineteenth-century Dutch text of the secret dispatch on the right.
