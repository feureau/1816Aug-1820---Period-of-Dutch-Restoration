# Transcription of IMG_1416.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1416.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Portfolio 5, No. 11)
- **Date Range:** 1817-09-08 to 1817-09-15
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Royal birth celebration in the Yogyakarta Kraton, economic analysis of riverine salt transit tariffs, and Resident Nahuys van Burgst’s foundational policy memorandum advocating European private agricultural enterprise (*landhuur*) to relieve aristocratic impoverishment. On 8 September 1817, the principal consort of Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV delivered a son (installed as Crown Prince, future Sultan Hamengkubuwana V); Resident Nahuys dispatched ceremonial birth gifts comprising three pieces of fine Bengal linen (valued at 36 Spanish Dollars / f. 250) and 24 bottles of wine to the Ratu. Carey notes archival data on salt imports from Gresik to Yogyakarta (citing P.H. van der Kemp, *Oost-Indië's Herstel in 1816*, 1911, p. 262 fn. 2), observing that five successive customs tollgates along the Bengawan Solo inflated salt prices to punitive levels, warning that a rigid Government Salt Monopoly would deprive poor Javanese peasants of subsistence, severing them from cheap local supplies from the Pacitan coast. On 15 September 1817, Nahuys dispatched his seminal memorandum to the Council of Finances (*Raad van Financiën*) advocating Western agricultural methods in the *Vorstenlanden*. Nahuys contended that expanding commercial cultivation was the only viable means to rescue the impoverished royal princes and nobles of Yogyakarta from ruin, proposing direct cash rental payments to landholders and cash wages to peasant day-laborers organized by private European planters, countering state-monopolistic objections with the maxim *"wat te veel bewijst, niets bewijst"* (what proves too much proves nothing).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, Sultan Hamengkubuwana V (infant prince), Ratu Kencana, P.H. van der Kemp.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Gresik, Bengawan Solo, Pacitan [Patjitan], Preanger Regencies [Priangan], Bengal.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Landhuur* [private land leasing], *Raad van Financiën* [Council of Finances], Salt Monopoly [Zoutmonopolie], tollgate tariffs [Bandar], forced deliveries [*contingenten*], Bengal linen, day laborers [*koeli*], cash wages.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 5, No. 11 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Collection, Djokjakarta General Dispatches, August–September 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Diplomatic correspondence, commercial expense vouchers, fiscal memoranda, and agrarian reform philosophy.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Captures the intellectual birth of the *landhuur* policy in Central Java, positioning European private capital as a paternalistic instrument to revitalize the shattered economy of the royal courts.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, the colonial Council of Finances in Batavia, and the Yogyakarta Kraton ruling elite.
