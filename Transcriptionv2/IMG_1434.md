- **Date Range:** 1997-01-31
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Scholarly correspondence regarding the historical identity of Kapitan Cina Tan Jin Sing (Raden Tumenggung Secadiningrat) during Resident Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst's 1818 colonial expedition to West Borneo (Kalimantan). Writing from Bovenden, Germany on 31 January 1997, Southeast Asian historian Dr. Mary Somers Heidhues consults Dr. Peter Carey regarding her research on Chinese Hakka gold miners in West Kalimantan. She notes that during Nahuys' mission as Special Government Commissioner to Borneo in late 1818, Dutch historian P.J. Veth (*Borneo's Wester-afdeeling*, Vol. II, pp. 55–56) records that Nahuys was accompanied by a Java-born Chinese dignitary who had assisted Governor-General Daendels during his 1810 expedition to Yogyakarta, who had been granted the priyayi title of "Setja Adi-ningrat" [Secadiningrat], elevated to the rank of Tumenggung, and endowed with an apanage estate. Veth noted that Secadiningrat served as colonial interpreter, though as an acculturated Hokkien (*peranakan*), he held a disparaging view of the rustic Hakka miners organized in local mining kongsis. Heidhues queries Carey on whether archival sources confirm that this "Setjo Adi-ningrat" was indeed the famous Yogyakarta Kapitan Cina Tan Jin Sing, noting the absence of any reference to a Borneo voyage in T.S. Werdoyo's biographical chronicle (*Tan Jin Sing: Dari Kapiten Cina Sampai Bupati Yogyakarta*, 1990).

  - **Key Entities (Places):** Bovenden (Germany), Oxford (Trinity College, UK), Yogyakarta [Djogja], West Kalimantan [Borneo's Wester-afdeeling / Pontianak / Sambas], Bangka.

Carey<br>Oxford University<br>Trinity College<br>Oxford OX1 3BH<br>United Kingdom | **Dear Peter,**<br><br>As a kind of continuation of my interest in Hakka tin miners on Bangka, I have been looking at the gold miners in West Kalimantan, also for the most part Hakkas. Although this may seem a long way from Java, I have found some information I would like to ask your opinion about.<br><br>**Nahuys' 1818 Borneo Mission & "Setja Adi-ningrat":**<br>• In 1818, Nahuys [Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst], who as you know had been Resident of Yogyakarta, was sent as Commissioner to Borneo.<br>• P.J. Veth in his *Borneo's Wester-afdeeling* (Vol. II, pp. 55–56) mentions that Nahuys was accompanied by a Chinese, born in Java, who had aided Daendels [Herman Willem Daendels] during his expedition to Yogyakarta and was given the Javanese title **"Setja Adi-ningrat" [Radèn Tumenggung Secadiningrat]**, raised to the rank of Temenggung, and given land.<br>• He was expected to act as an interpreter, and apparently performed good service.<br>• However, being a **Hokkien Chinese and not a Hakka, he had a low opinion of Borneo's Chinese**.<br><br>**Historiographical Identification with Tan Jin Sing:**<br>• This person seems to be identical with **"Tan Jin Sing"**.<br>• I found nothing in the biography of him by Werdoyo [T.S. Werdoyo, 1990] about his going to Borneo (small wonder), but I wonder if you ever came across any references to this excursion and could identify "Setjo Adi-ningrat" as Tan — or not.<br><br>This is a trivial matter, and I know you are more than busy with your own responsibilities. But I thought there might be a chance you would know if this person was the same as Tan Jin Sing or not. In any case, he certainly was a loyal servant of the Dutch.<br><br>If you are too busy to reply, or have no information, please just ignore my request.<br><br>Sincerely yours,<br>**Mary**<br>*(You may or may not remember me from ECIMS and EUROSEAS meetings).* |
