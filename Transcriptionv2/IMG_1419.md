## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1419.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Portfolio 5, No. 11: Djogjakarta Letters, November–December 1817)
- **Date Range:** 1817-09-03 to 1817-11-04
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic policy critique of the inland customs tollgate (*bandar*) and market bazaar (*pasar*) tax-farming system submitted by Resident Nahuys van Burgst in response to Governor-General Van der Capellen's memorandum of 3 September 1817. On 4 November 1817, Nahuys detailed the arbitrary, extractive nature of the tollgates, noting that toll rates fluctuated wildly based on the personal caprice of the tollgate keeper (*bandar*), with disproportionately heavy levies placed on minor rural roads heavily trafficked by poor Javanese peasants during daylight hours. Critically, Nahuys exposed institutionalized racial discrimination in tariff structures: native Javanese petty traders and peasants paid substantially higher toll rates than Chinese commercial syndicates, who enjoyed preferential reduced rates, shifting the tax burden away from large merchants and onto subsistence producers. Nahuys adamantly argued that granting Chinese tax-farmers privileges over native Javanese landholders provoked intense, dangerous social discontent throughout the countryside; while rationalizing tariffs and lowering rates would cause an immediate reduction in colonial toll-farm revenues, this loss would be amply compensated by political pacification and rural tranquility. He proposed introducing transparent published tariff schedules, a declining rate scale for long-distance transit through multiple barriers (*rangkah* / *salaran*), and mandatory vernacular explanations to prevent Chinese tollkeepers from exploiting widespread peasant illiteracy.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Chinese tax-farmers [Bandar], Javanese peasant traders.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta [Solo], Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bandar* [tollgate keeper / customs post], *Pasar* [bazaar tax-farm], Tollgate discrimination, peasant illiteracy, transit tariff regulation, *Rangkah* / *Salaran* [subordinate customs barriers], *Vorstenlanden*.
