# Transcription of IMG_1456.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1456.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Kabinet Dossier No. 2065: Ingekomen Berigten over het Inlandsch Onderwijs, 1831–1832 / Bawean, Madura, and Surabaya)
- **Date Range:** 1831-10-12 to 1831-10-26
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Educational surveys and policy blueprints across the offshore islands and Eastern Java, covering Madura, Bawean, and the Surabaya Education Commission. In Madura, the Sultan of Madura rejected colonial government schools, remaining fiercely committed to traditional custom (*adat*), while Sumenep and Pamekasan recorded 34 Islamic pesantrens with defective vernacular instruction. On the isolated island of Bawean (26 October 1831), 109 village *langgars* operated twice daily teaching Qur'anic recitation (*ngaji*), requiring 3 to 4 years to complete (*khatam* / *tamat*), where teachers were rewarded with batik cloth worth f. 2–4; affluent parents provided 30 to 50 Reals to send sons for advanced Islamic legal studies in Pasuruan. Due to an acute shortage of literate native clerks, the district officer proposed consolidating the langgars into 7 or 8 centralized government schools (two at Sangkapura, and one each at Nuwari, Sukameng, Promahan, Kepoh, Panwu, and Lebak), accommodating 100 to 200 pupils in bamboo pavilions financed by a maritime export levy of 8 cents per *loyang* on Madurese boat trade. The document concludes with the Surabaya Education Commission's blueprint: school hours set strictly from 8:00–11:00 a.m. and 2:00–4:00 p.m. to avoid Islamic prayer times (*salat*); salaried teachers (f. 10 to f. 15 monthly) endowed with the aristocratic priyayi rank of *Demang* and entitled to carry an official state umbrella (*payung*); free tuition for sons of regents; free government textbooks; and making vernacular literacy a mandatory prerequisite for native administrative office.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Johannes van den Bosch, Sultan of Madura, Resident of Surabaya, District Officer of Bawean, Regents of Surabaya and Gresik, Islamic village teachers.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Madura, Sumenep [Sumënëp], Pamekasan, Bawean Island, Sangkapura, Pasuruan, Surabaya, Gresik.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Langgar* schooling (109 schools in Bawean), *Mëngaji* [Quranic recitation], *Tamat* [graduation / khatam], Pasuruan Islamic legal studies, maritime export levy, secular school schedule (avoiding prayer times), *Demang* rank for teachers, State umbrella (*payung*), civil service qualification.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Kabinet Dossier No. 2065 (1831–1832 Returns on Native Education).
* **Document Nature:** Regional administrative reports, island educational surveys, fiscal tariff proposals, and institutional school charters.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates colonial efforts to extend institutional education to remote maritime frontiers like Bawean, balancing secular priyayi incentives with strict respect for Islamic prayer rituals.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Governor-General Van den Bosch, the Surabaya Education Commission, and Madurese traditional rulers.
