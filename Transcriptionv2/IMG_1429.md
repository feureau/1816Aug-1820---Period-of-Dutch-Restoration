# Transcription of IMG_1429.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1429.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (KITLV H 698b: Algemeen Verslag Jogja [AvJ], 1818: Ingekomen en Uitgaande Brieven)
- **Date Range:** 1818-01-05 to 1818-12-19
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Peasant economic resistance to the colonial Salt Monopoly, royal bupati appointments by Patih Danureja IV, liquidation auction of Kyai Murma Wijaya's Kepundhung/Bunder estate, and the fiscal balance sheet of Yogyakarta's royal annuities and toll revenues. On 4 December 1818, Assistant Resident R.C.N. d'Abo notified the Commissioners-General that southern coastal villagers strongly opposed abandoning artisanal salt-making in exchange for government cash indemnities; because the arid coastal belt provided negligible agricultural employment in sawahs or tegals, villagers preferred active production to enforced idleness, while Yogyakarta consumers universally rejected northern salt (*Pasisir* salt) due to taste and price. On 5 January 1818, Nahuys petitioned Batavia to ratify administrative appointments of several Yogyakarta bupatis made by Patih Danureja IV. On 1 June, Nahuys reported the successful public auction of Kyai Murma's Kepundhung (Bunder) estate: proceeds were prudently split between a maintenance fund for Murma's exile in Ambon and financial loans for his extended kin (mitigating the customary Javanese legal principle of collective family punishment). On 19 December 1818, writing from Semarang, d'Abo reported the introduction of the new transit taxation system on Yogyakarta products, noting that customs tollgates in the annexed districts of Jabarangkah and Ledok yielded 28,200 guilders. He cataloged the heavy recurring financial obligations of the colonial treasury: monthly stipends to Paku Alam I (1,650 guilders), maintenance of the Sultan's Bodyguard (530 guilders), biannual royal payments to Sultan HB IV (110,000 Spanish Dollars paid on 1 February and 1 August), semi-annual birds' nests revenues from Bagelèn (440 Sp. D.), and Pacitan district land taxes (*pajeg*).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Robbert Christiaan Nicolaas d'Abo, Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Radèn Adipati Danureja IV, Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, Paku Alam I (Pangéran Natakusuma), Kyai Murma Wijaya, Demang of Pacitan, Commissioners-General.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Yogya], South Coast [Segara Kidul], Kepundhung [Bunder], Semarang, Jabarangkah, Ledok, Bagelèn, Pacitan.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Government Salt Monopoly, peasant resistance to cash indemnity, Patih administration, public auction of confiscated estate (*boedel*), collective family guilt (*hukum pidana adat*), *Jabarangkah* & *Ledok* tollgates, royal allowances (*pensioenen*), Sultan's European Bodyguard, semi-annual treaty payments, *Pajeg* [land tax].
