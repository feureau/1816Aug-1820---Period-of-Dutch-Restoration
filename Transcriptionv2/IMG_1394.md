- **Date Range:** 1817-09-19 to 1817-11-04
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Proposals for European private enterprise in Pacitan and colonial condemnation of fiscal discrimination at road tollgates. On 19 September 1817, Resident Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst transmitted an offer from a prominent European resident of Yogyakarta, C.H. Coster, to lease the entire regency tax-farm of Pacitan for a period of ten years. Nahuys strongly endorsed Coster's bid, arguing that Pacitan was so economically underdeveloped that an average peasant household failed to generate even one single Java Rupee in annual revenue, and maintaining that the introduction of an energetic European entrepreneur into that isolated, southern coastal district would stimulate regional commerce, improve agriculture, and integrate the periphery into the colonial market economy. In a separate dispatch of 4 November 1817, Nahuys lodged a fierce protest with the Commissarissen-Generaal against the glaring structural inequities of the internal tollgate system (*bandar*), pointing out that indigenous Javanese peasants were forced to pay significantly higher transit duties on basic food supplies and market produce than wealthy Chinese merchants, who enjoyed preferential contract rates.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], C.H. Coster [C.M. Coster], Commissarissen-Generaal [C.G.].
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Yogya], Pacitan, South Coast (*Segara Kidul*), Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** European estate leasing (*landhuur*), tax farming (*pacht*), *Bandar* [customs tollgate], discriminatory tariff rates, peasant household poverty, Java Rupees [J.R.].
