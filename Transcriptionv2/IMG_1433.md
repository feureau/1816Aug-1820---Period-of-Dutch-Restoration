# Transcription of IMG_1433.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1433.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5: Nahuys van Burgst Papers / Surakarta Letters, April 1818)
- **Date Range:** 1818-04-01 to 1818-04-30
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Forensic analysis of Pangéran Prangwedana's (Mangkunagara II) financial insolvency, colonial divide-and-rule diplomacy, dynastic succession theories, and the diplomatic blocking of an alliance marriage between the Kraton and the Mangkunagaran house. Resident Van Prehn noted that Prangwedana's severe financial distress was driven by lavish expenditures on gold braid, European uniforms, and military regalia for his extensive circle of relations, exacerbated by poor estate administration managed entirely by his patih. The colonial strategy aimed to maintain Prangwedana in a state of financial dependence on the Dutch government while preserving his political independence from the Sunan, acting as a crucial intermediary between Batavia and the courts. To bind Mangkunagara closer to colonial authority, Van Prehn recommended granting the title of *Pangéran Timur* to his 15-year-old youngest son, Raden Mas Serengrat. Addressing succession principles, the dispatch contrasts European primogeniture (eldest son succession) with Javanese practice, where the reigning sovereign selected his preferred heir, fostering chronic intrigue. When Sunan PB IV sought to arrange a dynastic marriage between his favorite son Pangéran Purbaya (future PB VII) and a daughter of Prangwedana, colonial officials actively intervened to block the union, fearing the Mangkunagaran military force would fall under anti-Dutch Kraton influence. Prangwedana complied with Dutch advice, disavowing any close alliance with the palace. Finally, the text records the political vacuum inside the Kraton following the 1816 banishment of Pangéran Mangkubumi to Ambon: deprived of his closest confidant, the Sunan turned to secret cabals with Cakranegara and grew hostile toward Crown Prince Sugandi (forcing him to reside outside the palace).
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II), Sunan Pakubuwana IV, Crown Prince of Surakarta (Sugandi / future Sunan Pakubuwana V), Pangéran Purbaya (future Sunan Pakubuwana VII), Raden Mas Serengrat (Pangéran Timur), Pangéran Mangkubumi (Surakarta), Resident Rijck van Prehn, Raden Tumenggung Cakranegara.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Surakarta [Solo / Sala], Mangkunagaran, Kraton Surakarta, Ambon, Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Financial dependence vs. political independence, Mangkunagaran military uniforms & gold braid, *Pangéran Timur* [princely title], Primogeniture vs. royal preference, dynastic alliance marriage, *Mangkubumi* exile to Ambon (1816), exclusion of Crown Prince from palace.

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portfolio 6, Part 5 (Leiden University Library: Nahuys van Burgst Papers, Surakarta Succession Minutes, April 1818).
