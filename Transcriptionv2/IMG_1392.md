## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1392.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (KITLV H 698b: AvJ 1817 [Yogya], May–July 1817 / Nahuys Dispatches from Yogyakarta and Surabaya)
- **Date Range:** 1817-05-01 to 1817-07-24
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Agrarian administration, venality of indigenous offices, the Pacitan pepper cultivation reform, and the judicial resolution of the Malang insurrection. On 1 May 1817, Resident Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst reported that the stagnation of export cash crops (coffee and pepper) in Central Java was caused by the arbitrary dismissals of village headmen (*bekel*, *demang*, *lurah*) by priyayi apanage-holders, who sold rural offices to the highest bidder; when Nahuys dismissed the Demang of Boyolali for corruption, two Javanese candidates immediately offered bribes of 500 Spanish Dollars for the post. To rehabilitate Pacitan, Nahuys proposed planting 150,000 pepper vines, granting a 50% tax remission, and guaranteeing a government purchase price of 3 Spanish Dollars per pikul. In July, following complaints from the Resident of Surabaya regarding cross-border raids on Malang mounted by bandits from Ngrawa [Tulungagung], Nahuys conducted an on-site inspection alongside Pangéran Prangwedana (Mangkunagara II), mobilizing 100 Surakarta court soldiers and 60 Mangkunagaran legionnaires to restore order. Writing from Surabaya on 10 July, Nahuys reported the capture of rebel ringleader Raden Jaladkara (son of Pangéran Purbanagara and son-in-law of Sunan Pakubuwana IV), who had previously served as a military officer (*panji*) under Pangéran Mangkubumi of Surakarta during the British period, and was now incarcerated in Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Pangéran Prangwedana / Mangkunagara II, Raden Jaladkara [P. Jaladara], Pangéran Purbanagara, Sunan Pakubuwana IV [PB IV], Pangéran Mangkubumi (Surakarta), Demang of Boyolali, Resident of Surabaya, Commissarissen-Generaal [Comm.-Gen.].
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Boyolali, Pacitan, Malang, Ngråwå [Ngrawa / Tulungagung], Pasuruan, Surabaya, Gagatan, Yogyakarta [Yogya], Surakarta [Solo], Batavia.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Bekel*, *Demang*, *Lurah*, venality of office (*ambtsverkoop*), coffee and pepper cultivation, tax remissions, Mangkunagaran Legion, *Panji* [military officer rank], prison in Batavia, Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** KITLV H 698b; transcripts of Yogyakarta Residency Dispatches (Mei–Juli 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Executive administrative reports, field inspection notes, and high-level police intelligence abstracts drafted during Nahuys's investigative mission to East Java.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates the socio-economic roots of rural instability—specifically the venal exploitation of the village headman tier by court aristocrats—paired with the forensic dismantling of the East Javanese priyayi conspiracy headed by Raden Jaladkara.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Pangéran Prangwedana, Surakarta court troops, Mangkunagaran legionnaires, and the Resident of Surabaya.
* **Chronological Entry Flow:** Entries arranged in dense chronological order spanning May through late July 1817 (`1 May 1817`, `6 July`, `23 July`, `24 July`, `10 July`).
* **Marginal Annotations:** Left column tracks dispatch dates, correspondents, and geographic loci (`Pacitan`, `[from Surabaya]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, administrative rubrics, and troop numbers on the left; verbatim socio-economic critiques, venality anecdotes, military deployments, and genealogical depositions on the right.
