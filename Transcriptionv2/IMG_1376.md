# Transcription of IMG_1376.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1376.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Arsip Nasional Republik Indonesia [ANRI], Djokjo Brieven Bundel 60: Jan–Feb 1817)
- **Date Range:** 1816-12-31 to 1817-01-24
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Continuing intelligence reports and administrative correspondence on the Umar Mahdi uprising, princely bodyguard protocols, currency supplies, and priyayi fugitive movements. An intercepted deposition reveals that all pilgrims (*haji*) who had journeyed to Mecca and Medina were summoned to return to Java to join Umar Mahdi's insurgent band (50 men, 14 women, armed with 50 pikes and displaying 10 white flags). A translated letter from Juma Tirta to Ngabèhi Kertaredja at Surakarta describes the quarterly secret assembly at desa Uneba/Jueba, where 50 followers in white robes and multi-colored turbans gathered around Umar Mahdi of Sambirata, who claimed authority through Arabic-inscribed mandates and miraculous apparitions of a heaven-sent mosque (*Mesjid Tiban*). The document further records the 12 January 1817 decree establishing formal military guards of honor for the Residents of Surakarta and Yogyakarta drawn from the elite princely corps (Prangwedana's Legion and the Pakualaman Corps); inter-residency dispatches from Pinket van Haak on fugitive village headmen seeking sanctuary as priyayi apprentices (*magang*) in Yogyakarta; the colonial government's formal warrant reimbursing the 50 Spanish Dollar capture expense; a consignment of 5,000 Java Rupees in copper coinage sent to alleviate Yogyakarta's liquidity crisis; and a dispatch from Resident Valck of Kedu tracking the flight of Jurawidjaya (dismissed Demang of Magelang) to the compound of Puspadiwiria, a mantri of Radèn Tumenggung Danureja IV.

  - **Key Entities (Places):** Sambirata [Masaran], Desa Uneba / Jueba, Bagelèn, Surakarta [Solo], Yogyakarta [Djogja], Kedhu [Magelang], Pekalongan [Desa Bruntjak], Mecca [Mekka], Medina.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** *Mesjid Tiban* [heaven-dropped mosque], *Haji* [Mecca pilgrims], white robes / turbans, *Umbul-umbul* [streamers/banners], *Magang* [unpaid priyayi apprentices], honor guard (*erewacht*), copper doits / Java Rupees [J.R.], Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.], *Djåwå* (1938 citation).

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI, Djokjo Brieven Bundel 60 (Januari–Februari 1817); supplemented by citations to journal *Djåwå* Jg. 18 (1938), p. 267.
* **Document Nature:** Archival dispatch calendar, military decrees, intelligence intercepts, and financial warrants transcribed from residency registries.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates how millenarian insurgency, princely military institutionalization, cross-border jurisdictional frictions, and currency scarcity dominated the administrative landscape of early 1817.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Dutch Residents (Nahuys, Pinket van Haak, Valck), indigenous princely auxiliary commanders (Prangwedana, Paku Alam I), court patihs (Danureja IV), and rural millenarian leaders (Umar Mahdi).
* **Header / Margin Rubrics:** Top margin notes capturing religious summonings and insurgent military strength.
* **Bibliographic Reference:** Carey notes in red ink on the left margin citing scholarly documentation (`[Red ink margin: Djåwå Jg. 18 (1938) p. 267]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Inter-residency communication dates, colonial decrees, and provenance notes on the left; verbatim abstracts, military counts, and administrative details on the right.
