# Transcription of IMG_1424.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1424.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (ANRI Djokjo Brieven Bundel 61: June–November 1818)
- **Date Range:** 1818-06-03 to 1818-06-25
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Official budget allocations for the Sultan of Yogyakarta, penal colonization of Banda following the abolition of the slave trade, transit of Kyai Murma Wijaya and Umar Mahdi to Ambon, rejection of return petitions from exiled princes Pangéran Mangkudiningrat I and Pangéran Mertasana, and technical specifications for copper coinage minted in Surabaya. In 1817, annual colonial compensation payments to Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV totaled f. 220,000 for ceded tollgates, market pasars, and edible birds' nests, alongside f. 12,800 to Paku Alam I, f. 10,105.15 for the European Dragoon corps, and f. 249,905.15 for the Sultan's personal bodyguard (reaching a grand disbursement of f. 492,810.30). By decree of 3 June 1818, facing acute labor deficits on Banda spice plantations caused by the abolition of the trans-oceanic slave trade, Batavia authorized the deportation of Javanese convicts and political exiles alongside their families to labor on the nutmeg groves. On 10 June, Kyai Murma and Umar Mahdi arrived under armed escort in Semarang for transport to Ambon, with Murma awarded an installation grant of f. 2,200 upon arrival (Decree No. 7, 9 June 1818). On 24 June, the Governor-General formally rejected petitions submitted by exiled princes Pangéran Arya Mangkudiningrat I and Pangéran Arya Mertasana seeking permission to return to Kertosono and Madiun, following Nahuys' secret warning of 16 June establishing that both had been captured bearing arms during the 1812 British storming of the Kraton and maintained dangerous dynastic followings. Finally, on 25 June 1818, the Commissioners-General enacted a proclamation regulating the minting of uniform copper stuivers (2, 1, and ½ stuivers) in Surabaya utilizing imported Japanese copper.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV, Paku Alam I (Pangéran Natakusuma), Sultan Hamengkubuwana II, Pangéran Arya Mangkudiningrat I, Pangéran Arya Mertasana, Kyai Murma Wijaya, Raden Mas Umar Mahdi, Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip baron van der Capellen, Governor of the Moluccas, Resident of Semarang.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djogja], Surakarta, Semarang, Ambon, Banda [Banda Neira], Kertosono [Kērtasana], Madiun, Surabaya, Japan.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Royal annuities / subsidies, European Bodyguard, Mangkubumi / Kasepuhan faction, Abolition of Slave Trade, penal labor on Banda spice estates, political exile (*verbanning*), Japanese copper coinage (*duiten* / stuivers), minting standards.

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** ANRI Djokjo Brieven Bundel 61 (Ingekomen en Uitgaande Brieven van de Residentie Jogjakarta, June–November 1818).
* **Document Nature:** Executive fiscal summaries, secret state resolutions (*Geheime Besluiten*), penal exile transfers, and monetary proclamations.
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Illustrates how high colonial politics managed the persistent threat of royal Pretenders and religious messianism through permanent Moluccan exile, while restructuring state finances and the currency supply.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Governor-General Van der Capellen, Governor of the Moluccas, Resident of Semarang, and the Kraton hierarchy.
* **Archival Header:** Top entries register the bundle details and annual court budget figures (`Yogya Res. Letters / Jan. - Jun. 1818 / [red ink: Dj. Br. 61 / June - November 1818]`).
* **Marginal Annotations:** Red ink margin headings identifying financial totals, Ambon exiles, royal petitions, and coinage regulations (`[red ink: f. 492,810.30 / Total f.]`, `[red ink: Kyai Murma in Ambon]`, `[red ink: Request of MD / MS to Return home.]`, `[red ink: copper coins.]`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, decree numbers, and financial categories on the left; verbatim summaries of royal subsidies, penal servitude in Banda, exile arrivals, rejected royal pleas, and Surabaya copper minting on the right.
