# Transcription of IMG_1399.jpg

## Metadata

- **Image File:** `jpg/IMG_1399.jpg`
- **Source:** Peter Carey Collection / Archival Research Notes (Leiden University Library BPL 616: Nahuys van Burgst Collection / Djocja Correspondence General, Feb 1817)
- **Date Range:** 1817-01-14 to 1817-02-06
- **Key Events & Entities:**
  - **Key Events:** Urban administration, monetary instability, royal subsidies, exile logistics, and a scathing forensic report on municipal policing in Yogyakarta during January–February 1817. On 23 January, European burgher Willem Driessen petitioned for age-related relief from municipal militia service. The residency recorded annual ceremonial festival disbursements of 2,451 Java Rupees across four official state celebrations (the King's Birthday, Puasa, Besar, and New Year). On 1 February, the administration confronted severe currency depreciation arising from the circulation of unbacked colonial paper money (*fiat money*); implementing Governor-General Van der Capellen's Resolution of 14 January, Resident Nahuys decreed that all tollgate (*bandar*) leases and opium tax-farms must be paid strictly in silver specie, with royal allowances to Paku Alam I and Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV (110,000 Java Rupees) disbursed likewise in silver to prevent speculative profiteering by Chinese currency exchangers. On 3 February, Raden Ayu Kencana Wulan departed Yogyakarta to join the exiled Sultan Sepuh (Hamengkubuwana II) in Ambon. Finally, a damning police report of 6 February revealed that security had deteriorated since the British administration; lock-ups were filthy and insecure, violent murders and robberies were daily occurrences, and captured highway dacoits (*kècu*) were routinely and rapidly released by Patih Danureja IV after brief, token incarcerations.
  - **Key Entities (Persons):** Major Huibert Gerard Nahuys van Burgst [NvB], Willem Driessen, Godert Alexander Gerard Philip van der Capellen [G.G.], Pangéran Natakusuma / Paku Alam I, Sultan Hamengkubuwana IV [HB IV], Sultan Hamengkubuwana II [Old Sultan], Raden Ayu Kencana Wulan [Radèn Kentjana Woeling], Raden Adipati Danureja IV [Patih], Chinese tax-farmers.
  - **Key Entities (Places):** Yogyakarta [Djocja], Ambon / Moluccas, Batavia, Vorstenlanden [V.L.], Leiden.
  - **Key Entities (Institutions / Terms):** Specie versus paper currency (*fiat money*), silver agio, royal treaty subsidies, tollgate and opium monopolies, *Bui* [indigenous prison/lock-up], municipal police corruption, *Kècu* [violent dacoits], King's Birthday (*Koningsdag*), Garèbèg Puwasa, Garèbèg Besar, Java Rupees [J.R.].

### Header & Archival Classification

* **Archival Series:** BPL 616, Portefeuille 5 (Universiteitsbibliotheek Leiden, Nahuys van Burgst Collection: Djocja Correspondence General, Februari 1817).
* **Document Nature:** Municipal petitions, fiscal currency resolutions, secretarial warrants, and internal residency police inspection reports (*policie-rapporten*).
* **Chronological / Thematic Trajectory:** Exposes the structural dysfunctions of early Restoration governance: the monetary chaos caused by depreciating paper guilders, the corrupt breakdown of the Danurejan judicial apparatus, and the systemic insecurity plaguing urban Yogyakarta.
* **Administrative & Military Actors:** Resident Nahuys van Burgst, Governor-General Van der Capellen, Grand Vizier Danureja IV, Paku Alam I, and royal consort Ratu Kencana Wulan.
* **Archival Header:** Top left registers shelfmark notations (`N VAN B / LEIDEN. / DJOCJA CORR (GENERAL) / 1817 (FEB) / N VAN B ARCHIVE LEIDEN.`).
* **Chronological Flow:** Left column tracks exact dispatch dates (`23 Jan 1817`, `1 Feb.`, `4 Feb.`, `3 Feb.`, `6 Feb.`).
* **Two-Column Organization:** Dates, monetary regulations, and legal rubrics on the left; verbatim burgher petitions, festival budgets, currency decrees, exile departures, and crime reports on the right.
